Opposition protests against the Chief Election Commissioner: second day of detentions, FIR over Akashvani Bhavan

Leader of the Opposition Rahul Gandhi said on Wednesday (7 October) that protests against Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar would not stop until he resigns over alleged "vote chori" (theft). Mr Gandhi was taken to the Barakhamba Road police station and released by 8 p.m.

  • Tuesday's march: police detained about 260 people, including 10 MPs, after the march from Parliament towards the Election Commission office. Supreme Court to examine plea to recall the 23 September split verdict on the Election Commissioners law

GS-II PolityJudiciary · ElectionsJustice Datta · Justice SharmaPrelims + Mains

The Supreme Court on Wednesday agreed to examine and take a call on hearing an application to recall a split verdict of 23 September in the challenge to the 2023 law on appointing Election Commissioners. Senior advocate Gopal Sankaranarayanan mentioned the plea before Chief Justice Surya Kant. It questions why Justice Satish Chandra Sharma did not disclose during several hearings that his son was a standing counsel for the Election Commission of India. The application was filed by Naman Sherstra.

  • Differing views: Justice Dipankar Datta, the lead judge, declined to refer the challenge to a Constitution Bench and observed that the 2023 law was prima facie wrong to ignore the Constitution Bench ruling in Anoop Baranwal (which had the Chief Justice of India as a neutral member of the selection panel). Justice Sharma favoured a reference.
  • Common order: both judges asked the Chief Justice of India to allot the case to a Constitution Bench. The application objects to that order but does not specifically challenge Justice Datta's separate opinion.
  • The 2023 law: it replaced the Chief Justice of India on the selection panel with a Union Minister nominated by the Prime Minister.

Supreme Court flags accessibility gaps for air passengers with disabilities

GS-II Social JusticeDisability · AviationRights of Persons with Disabilities ActPrelims + Mains

A three-judge Bench headed by Chief Justice Surya Kant issued notice to the Ministry of Civil Aviation, the Directorate General of Civil Aviation, the Airports Authority of India, the Central Industrial Security Force and the Digi Yatra Foundation on a petition by Turab Chimthanawala, a Mumbai-based company secretary and advocate with a benchmark disability, Leber's Congenital Amaurosis. The Court will ask why millions of passengers with disabilities continue to face "humiliation, barriers, broken equipment, and digital exclusion".

  • Numbers cited: India is the world's third-largest aviation market, with annual passenger traffic above 350 million; domestic traffic has grown about 10-12% a year. Using the 2.21% disability prevalence from Census 2011, the petition estimates over seven million air passengers with disabilities a year (petitioner's estimate).
  • Gaps alleged: wheelchair-dependent passengers offloaded; visually impaired passengers forced to use ambulifts; in-flight systems with no accessibility settings; "silent airports" with no audio, visual or WhatsApp alerts; airline apps and sites that do not support screen readers; no clear grievance mechanism.

India's cement footprint stands in the way of net zero

GS-III EnvironmentClimate · IndustryCement · clinker · RDFPrelims + Mains

By 2047 India plans to be a developed economy and by 2070 a net-zero emitter. Construction is a big source of emissions: the sector's footprint is put at 35-40% of global greenhouse gas emissions, and cement alone 7-11%, almost double aviation. Cement is "hard to abate" because, besides the heat needed, calcination of limestone releases carbon dioxide.

  • Making cement: limestone is mixed with clay and heated to about 1,450 degrees Celsius; heating accounts for 35-40% of cement-making emissions and the rest comes from the chemical breakdown of limestone. The by-product clinker is responsible for 53-65% of emissions. India emits 0.62 tonne of carbon dioxide per tonne of cement; a roadmap by The Energy and Resources Institute and the Global Cement and Concrete Association seeks 0.56 by 2030 and 0.51 by 2047, while output rises from 334 million tonnes (2019-20) to 1,546 million tonnes in 2070.
  • Solutions: the NITI Aayog roadmap wants a 20% thermal substitution of coal by refuse-derived fuel by 2030 (a cumulative cut of about 80 million tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent); cutting clinker by blending slag or fly ash (the International Energy Agency says replacing a tonne of clinker avoids 0.83 tonne of carbon dioxide); carbon capture. India's clinker-to-cement ratio of about 67.5% is already below the global 77%, but practical substitution cannot exceed about 40%.
  • Cement types: ordinary Portland cement has over 95% clinker, Portland pozzolana cement 65-80% and Portland slag cement 35-64%; builders prefer the first because it gains strength in a week versus about twice that for the others.
  • Alternatives: lime and lime-surkhi mortars, compressed earth blocks and rammed earth, cactus-juice binders, and Himachal's dry-masonry Kath Kuni; but experts say none can replace reinforced concrete in a ten-storey building. Hidden carbon costs include clearing forests and topsoil to mine limestone found 30-60 metres down in Karnataka, Chhattisgarh and Andhra Pradesh.

Op-ed: property taxation needs structural reform

Debarpita Roy of the National Institute of Urban Affairs writes that India's cumulative property tax is only 0.15-0.2% of GDP, against 0.6% in middle-income and 0.3% in low-income countries, though property tax is the main own revenue of municipal bodies and the basis of their creditworthiness. Two recent studies — a working paper on Chennai by Nobel laureate Esther Duflo and co-authors, and the author's research on Bengaluru, Pune and Ghaziabad — find that technology and process reforms yield poor results without structural reform.

  • People: municipal human resources matter; a municipality's own assessors do better than outsourced assessors, who lack institutional memory.
  • Elections: "base rate" revisions did not happen in the three cities when there were no elected councils; delayed municipal elections mean infrequent revision and poor buoyancy. By contrast, the stamp duty-linked circle rate is revised by the State regularly.
  • Accounts: poor financial reporting makes credible collection-efficiency estimates hard. The author urges more staff, timely elections and public financial data.

Uttarakhand’s final roll after SIR: 71.59 lakh electors, nearly 10% below the pre-SIR level

GS-II PolityElectoral rolls · SIRASDD · Form 6 · ECINetPrelims

Uttarakhand’s final electoral roll, published on 3 October, has 71.59 lakh electors against 79,60,762 when the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) began in June, a decline of nearly 10% (Chief Electoral Officer B.V.R.C. Purushottam).

  • Steps: first draft roll on 14 July (71,33,785 names); claims and objections till 13 August; hearings till 28 September. Since the draft, 1,04,708 names were deleted and over 1.29 lakh added, so the roll is up about 25,000; 1,29,872 are newly enrolled.
  • Profile: 34,21,036 women, 37,37,701 men and 212 third-gender electors; 72,656 aged 18-19; 1,22,892 aged above 80; 12,543 polling stations.
  • ASDD: many voters fall in the “uncollectable absent, shifted, dead or duplicate” category: Dehradun 1,86,008; Udham Singh Nagar 1,77,673; Haridwar 1,30,203.
  • Next: a special drive; left-out voters may apply on Form 6 online via the ECINet app or offline through the Booth Level Officer (BLO). The Congress claimed 8.5 lakh deleted in the final list plus 4.5 lakh pre-SIR, “13 lakh” in all.

Why did J&K’s Statehood resolution cause a row? The 2000 autonomy resolution and a clash with the bureaucracy

GS-II PolityFederalism · Union TerritoriesArticle 370 · Reorganisation ActMains

An explainer on the J&K Assembly’s resolution of 28 September seeking restoration of Statehood, which the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) did not vote on.

  • Vote: 61 of 90 members voted for it. The BJP objected to wording that placed it “in addition to” the resolutions of 26 June 2000 (autonomy) and 6 November 2024 (special status), and moved to remove Speaker Abdul Rahim Rather.
  • 2000 resolution: restore the pre-1953 autonomy; Union jurisdiction limited to defence, foreign affairs and communications; Governor as Sadr-e-Riyasat and Chief Minister as Wazir-e-Azam; no Article 356; separate Election Commission and CAG. Prime Minister Atal Bihari Vajpayee rejected it and the Union Cabinet called it an attempt to “set the clock back”.
  • Bureaucracy: Chief Secretary Atal Dulloo and Law Secretary Achal Sethi wrote to the Speaker advising against the resolution as sub judice; Omar Abdullah said he was not shown the letters and blamed the Union Territory status.
  • Chief Minister’s caution: Statehood should not be linked to a complete end of militancy.

BC quota in local body polls: State files SLP against the High Court’s 1 October order

GS-II PolityReservation · Local bodiesArticle 243-D(6) · 50% ceilingMains

Andhra Pradesh filed a Special Leave Petition in the Supreme Court challenging the 1 October High Court order on Backward Classes reservation in local body elections.

  • Order: the High Court set aside government orders to the extent that aggregate vertical reservation exceeded 50%.
  • State’s case: Articles 243-D(6) (panchayats) and 243-T(6) (municipalities) enable reservation for BCs; political reservation is distinct from reservation in education and jobs; the Unified Family Survey (G.O.Rt.No.207, 23 December 2025) provides contemporaneous empirical data as required by Vikas Kishanrao Gawali v. State of Maharashtra.
  • Law: the AP Panchayat Raj Act, 1994 (amended 1995) provides “not less than 34%” for BCs; G.O.Ms.No.105 restored 34%.
🎬 BC quota in local body elections

📌 Static GK

The 73rd and 74th Amendments (1992) inserted Parts IX and IX-A. Article 243-D reserves seats in panchayats for SCs, STs and women and lets States reserve for backward classes. The 50% ceiling on reservation comes from Indra Sawhney (1992); the “triple test” for local-body Other Backward Classes (OBC) quota came in Vikas Kishanrao Gawali (2021).

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • Art 243-D(6) and 243-T(6) – BC reservation in local bodies.

flydubai FZ 1073: UAE calls it a “terrorist attack” by the co-pilot; Indian captain Smit Machchhar a hero

GS-III SecurityAviation security · West AsiaTabuk · Crash axeMains

On 30 September flydubai flight FZ 1073 from the United Arab Emirates to Israel was diverted to Tabuk, Saudi Arabia, after the co-pilot attacked the captain, Indian national Smit Machchhar, a former SpiceJet pilot hired by flydubai in 2022.

  • Findings so far: the United Arab Emirates (UAE) Prosecutor General said the co-pilot tried to carry out a terrorist attack with a crash axe and to take control of the aircraft; initial passenger accounts spoke of a pocket knife. A source identified him as Hammam al-Hammami, an Omani national; a regional official said Oman had barred him from flying over concerns about extremist views before flydubai hired him; a Gulf diplomat said he passed standard checks.
  • Flight: the aircraft descended from about 34,000 ft to 19,000 ft, climbed, descended again and levelled at about 15,000 ft; ground images showed a large part of the tail rudder missing. More than 170 passengers and crew were on board.
  • Reactions: flydubai suspended all flights to and from Israel; the UAE leads the investigation; Dubai’s Crown Prince visited the captain in Abu Dhabi.

Rising child leprosy cases challenge India’s 2027 goal: 3,832 child cases in 2025-26

GS-II HealthLeprosy · NLEPGrade-2 disability · 2027 goalPrelims

India recorded 3,832 child leprosy cases in 2025-26, 91.6% above the Centre’s target of 2,000, with 37 children diagnosed with Grade-2 disability (G2D), pointing to delayed detection and ongoing transmission.

  • National picture: 91,783 new cases in 2025-26; prevalence 0.56 per 10,000; 1,945 patients (2.12%) with G2D; G2D rate 1.34 per million against a target of below one.
  • Global: India reported the most new cases in the WHO’s 2025 update (91,783), then Brazil (22,901) and Indonesia (16,292) — together 78.3% of cases globally.
  • Hotspots: districts with prevalence above one per 10,000: 23 in Chhattisgarh, 21 in Jharkhand, 18 each in Maharashtra and Odisha, 10 in Madhya Pradesh.
  • Disease: leprosy (Hansen’s disease) is caused mainly by Mycobacterium leprae, affects skin and peripheral nerves, spreads by prolonged close contact and is curable with multidrug therapy.

After great apes, African elephants self-medicate too

GS-III ScienceAnimal behaviour · EthnobotanyZoopharmacognosy · Traditional knowledgePrelims

A paper in Scientific Reports found elephants in Kenya’s Mount Elgon region use 39 natural resources — 35 plant species and 4 soil or clay types — apparently as medicine, learning from local experts and passing the knowledge to offspring.

  • Concept: zoopharmacognosy; examples include orangutans balming wounds, chimpanzees eating bitter leaves and dolphins rubbing on corals.

India’s river interlinks may create invasive-species “highways”

GS-III EnvironmentWater · BiodiversityRiver-linking · ATREEMains

Researchers at ATREE (Bengaluru) say the National River-Linking Project — proposing to link 37 basins and shift about 174 billion cubic metres of water while generating about 34,000 MW — could let invasive species spread between basins.

  • Findings: all Northeast and peninsular basins are more vulnerable than Central and North India; the Lower Ganga hosts 13-15 invasive species and could seed the Mahanadi, home to 69 threatened species.
  • Other risks: pathogens, fish migration, reduced sediment (for example in the Ken under the Ken-Betwa link) and downstream flows.
  • Policy: 15 freshwater invasives were assessed; across systems 350-400 species are at different stages of invasion; India needs an invasive-species policy.

Iraq’s militias within the military: Popular Mobilisation Forces after the U.S. withdrawal

GS-II International RelationsWest Asia · IraqPMF · Kataib HezbollahMains

The Profiles page explains Iraq’s Popular Mobilisation Forces (PMF) as U.S. troops completed their withdrawal on 30 September, 23 years after the invasion began.

  • Origin: Shia armed groups were brought under the PMF umbrella to fight the Islamic State; after its defeat they refused to disarm. A 2016 law made the PMF an independent organisation within the Iraqi armed forces, but factions kept separate command structures.
  • Size: more than three dozen militias, about 240,000 fighters and an annual budget of $3.4 billion. Iran-backed factions include the Badr Organisation, Kataib Hezbollah and Harakat Hezbollah al-Nujaba; Muqtada al-Sadr’s Saraya al-Salam is aligned but tense.
  • War context: after the U.S. and Israel began bombing Iran on 28 February 2026 the militias carried out about 600 attacks on U.S. facilities, per U.S. officials. Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi moved the demobilisation deadline to June 2027; Kataib Hezbollah and Nujaba reject disarmament.

How is AI reshaping Indian IT companies’ ‘benches’? Wipro’s new policy explained

GS-III EconomyIT services · EmploymentBench · Utilisation · AIMains

The bench is staff not assigned to a billable client project. Wipro changed its bench policy from 1 October: staff in Band 2 and below can stay unallocated for up to 180 days; Band 3 and above for 45 days and those with disabilities 75 days.

  • Trend: average bench time at large firms is estimated at 35-45 days in 2025 against 45-60 days in FY20-21; bench share 2-5% against 10-15% earlier.
  • History: Wipro cut its bench from about 11,000 to 7,000 in 2009; Infosys launched a “Zero Bench” programme in 2015; Persistent created TeXT.
  • Artificial Intelligence (AI): deployability now depends on skills; Tata Consultancy Services (TCS) reports over 2.7 lakh staff with advanced AI/ML proficiency.

1. Delhi clampdown on anti-CEC protests: prohibitory orders, mass detentions and Supreme Court hearing

GS-II PolityFundamental RightsElections / SIRPrelims + Mains

Youth and student groups — led by the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP), Left student bodies (AISA, SFI, AISF) and civil-society groups — called a protest at Jantar Mantar demanding the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar over alleged irregularities in the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls.

  • Delhi Police sources: ~2,500 detained during the day, plus ~1,000 in overnight preventive action; ~600 detained in Mumbai (incl. Tushar Gandhi).
  • Four former Chief Ministers detained — Mamata Banerjee, Pinarayi Vijayan, Arvind Kejriwal, Manik Sarkar.
  • Over 20,000 security personnel; 45 Metro stations shut, 200+ trains cancelled, Internet shutdown.
  • Prohibitory orders under Section 163 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) in New Delhi district.
  • CJP leaders were taken off Air India flight AI 2428 by Delhi Police with CISF; Air India wrote to BCAS citing the Aircraft (Security) Rules, 2023 (airlines control aircraft access).
  • A habeas corpus petition under Article 32 was filed; a Supreme Court Bench (Justices Joymalya Bagchi & V. Mohana) hears it in a special Sunday session, along with a contempt plea on the transport shutdown.
  • Lawyers detained outside the SC were released after the SCBA and SCAORA approached CJI Surya Kant.
  • UN OHCHR expressed concern over "mass detentions"; India's Permanent Mission in Geneva called it "unwarranted interference" and said rights are subject to reasonable restrictions.
  • BJP president Nitin Nabin accused the INDIA bloc and CJP of trying to destabilise the country; Opposition leaders called the crackdown an attack on dissent.
🎬 Legal pathway: from prohibitory order to habeas corpus
Former SC judge Abhay S. Oka: grounds must be communicated under Art 22 even when restraint is called "detention" rather than arrest.

📌 Static GK link

  • BNSS, 2023 replaced the CrPC (in force from 1 July 2024). Sec 163 order: valid up to 2 months; State govt may extend up to 6 months.
  • Article 324 – Election Commission; CEC can be removed only like an SC judge (Art 324(5)).
  • Habeas corpus can be filed by any person on behalf of the detenu; available against both State and private persons.
  • BCAS – under Ministry of Civil Aviation; CISF – CAPF under MHA, guards major airports.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • Sec 163 BNSS (in force 1 Jul 2024) replaced Sec 144 CrPC; order valid up to 2 months, State Govt can extend up to 6 months.
  • Art 22: protection against arrest & detention; clauses (1)–(2) do not apply to preventive detention. Art 19(1)(b) = assemble peaceably without arms; limits under 19(3).
  • Habeas corpus: Art 32 (SC) / Art 226 (HC); lies against State and private persons; any person can file for the detenu.
  • Anuradha Bhasin (2020): restrictions must be necessary & proportionate. Art 324: Election Commission; CEC removed like an SC judge.

UPSC 📝 Mains (GS)

Q. "Prohibitory orders under Sec 163 BNSS are increasingly a routine tool rather than an emergency power." Discuss with reference to Art 19 and the proportionality doctrine. (150 words)

Answer skeleton: Intro – Sec 163 vs old Sec 144; State duty to maintain order. Body – Art 19(1)(b) vs 19(3); proportionality test (Anuradha Bhasin); Art 22 grounds of detention; concerns: mass detention, Metro/Internet shutdown, UN OHCHR criticism vs India's "reasonable restrictions" stand. Way forward – reasoned written orders, time-bound review, judicial oversight, SOPs for protests. Conclusion – order and dissent must be balanced.

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

  • Constitution basics asked often in Gr-1/Gr-2 Polity: Arts 19, 21, 22, 32, 226; difference between preventive detention and arrest.
  • Link to the State: State Election Commission (Arts 243K / 243ZA) conducts local-body polls; ECI (Art 324) conducts Parliament/Assembly polls and the SIR of rolls (A.P. SIR began 15 Jul 2026).

🎬 Proposed BC reservation by local body (Commission report)

⚖️ The Supreme Court "Triple Test" for OBC quota in local bodies

① A dedicated commission for contemporaneous, rigorous empirical inquiry into backwardness in local bodies → ② specify the proportion of reservation local-body-wise → ③ total of SC + ST + OBC must not exceed 50%.
Cases: K. Krishna Murthy v. UoI (2010), Vikas Kishanrao Gawali v. State of Maharashtra (2021), Suresh Mahajan v. State of M.P. (2022).
Constitution: Art 243D (reservation in Panchayats), Art 243T (reservation in Municipalities); 243K/243ZA – State Election Commission. A.P. SEC: A.C. Punetha.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • Triple test (K. Krishna Murthy 2010; Vikas Gawali 2021; Suresh Mahajan 2022): (1) dedicated commission, (2) proportion local-body-wise, (3) SC+ST+OBC ≤ 50%.
  • Art 243D – reservation in Panchayats; Art 243T – in Municipalities; 243K / 243ZA – State Election Commissions.

1. World's first working nuclear clocks (Thorium-229)

GS-III S&TPhysics
  • Two papers in Nature: teams from China (Beijing & Shanghai) and Austria–Germany built clocks using the nucleus of thorium-229.
  • Atomic clocks use electron transitions (easily disturbed by fields/temperature); the nucleus is shielded by the electron cloud → potentially more stable.
  • Most nuclei need X-/gamma-rays to excite; Th-229 has an unusually low transition energy reachable with an ultraviolet laser, because the strong nuclear and electromagnetic forces nearly cancel.
  • Chinese team embedded Th-229 in calcium fluoride crystals; a feedback loop corrects laser drift.
  • European team compared it with a ytterbium atomic clock to hunt for ultralight dark matter — no signal found.
🎬 How a nuclear clock "ticks"

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • Nuclear clocks use the nucleus of thorium-229 (low transition energy, reachable with an ultraviolet laser); Chinese team embedded Th-229 in CaF₂ crystals; results in Nature.
  • Atomic clock (electron transitions) vs nuclear clock (nuclear transition, shielded by the electron cloud). Compared with a ytterbium clock to search for ultralight dark matter – none found.

2. El Niño may hit Tamil Nadu differently from the rest of India

GS-I GeographyClimatologyMonsoon
  • Agencies are tracking a "super" El Niño — east Pacific waters off Peru/Ecuador ~2 °C above average (normal El Niño ~0.5 °C).
  • El Niño usually means deficient southwest monsoon over India, but can supercharge the northeast monsoon over Tamil Nadu (e.g. 2015: Chennai got 1,522 mm in 4 days, 1–4 Dec).
  • Why: (1) pressure difference between Bay of Bengal and Arabian Sea creates a "wind tunnel" pushing moist air into coastal T.N.; (2) warmer Bay waters increase evaporation, released by Nov–Dec cyclonic circulations.
  • IMD reports a neutral IOD; southern Bay ~1 °C warmer (Sept 2026). The East India Coastal Current (EICC) reverses seasonally; an anomalous westward equatorial flow seen in 2026.
  • EU & Canada announced "OceanEye" — a real-time ocean data effort — at the UNGA.

1. Physics: Francis Halzen – the IceCube Neutrino Observatory

Nobel PhysicsAstrophysics
  • Belgian-American physicist; proposed IceCube with John G. Learned (1987–88); funded mainly by the U.S. National Science Foundation; completed 2011 after 7 years.
  • 5,160 optical sensors on 86 cables, 1.4–2.4 km deep in Antarctic ice (~1 km³) at the South Pole; holes melted with hot-water jets.
  • Neutrinos: no charge, tiny mass, travel in straight lines (unlike charged cosmic rays deflected by magnetic fields) → point back to sources.
  • Detection: a neutrino hits a nucleus → charged particles move faster than light in ice → Cherenkov radiation (blue glow).
  • Findings: 2013 – first astrophysical high-energy neutrinos; 2017 – neutrino from a blazar galaxy; 2022 – excess neutrinos from NGC 1068 (46 million ly).
🎬 Catching a neutrino in Antarctic ice
Of ~1 lakh neutrinos recorded yearly above an energy cut-off, only ~100 come from astrophysical sources.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • IceCube (South Pole): 5,160 optical sensors on 86 cables, 1.4–2.4 km deep, ~1 km³ of ice; completed 2011. Detects Cherenkov radiation. Milestones: 2013 first astrophysical neutrinos; 2017 blazar; 2022 NGC 1068.
  • Neutrinos: no charge, tiny mass, travel in straight lines → point back to sources.

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

2. Chemistry: Henri Kagan & Kenso Soai – amplifying molecular "handedness"

Nobel ChemistryChiralityPharma
  • Chirality (Greek kheir = hand): an object non-superimposable on its mirror image; mirror forms = enantiomers; 50:50 mix = racemate.
  • Life shows homochirality: amino acids are L-form, DNA sugars are D-form. Pasteur (1848) saw bacteria consume only one form of tartaric acid.
  • Drugs: thalidomide tragedy (1960s); ethambutol (TB) – (S,S) form ~500× more potent than (R,R).
  • Charles Frank (1953) model: autocatalysis + enantioselective catalysis + mutual antagonism.
  • Kagan (1986): a metal catalyst holding two ligands from a 75:25 mix gives a product ~90:10 — purity can be amplified (non-linear effect).
  • Soai (1995): asymmetric autocatalysis — product is its own catalyst; 5% excess → 90% in five rounds; 0.00005% → >99.5% in three rounds (~6,30,000-fold); even achiral start gives one hand by chance.
🎬 Kagan's amplification & Soai's autocatalysis

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • Chirality: enantiomers = non-superimposable mirror images; 50:50 mix = racemate; life uses L-amino acids and D-sugars (homochirality).
  • Kagan (1986) non-linear effect; Soai (1995) asymmetric autocatalysis. Drug link: thalidomide, ethambutol.

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

3. Physiology or Medicine: Deisseroth, Hegemann & Nagel – optogenetics

Nobel MedicineNeuroscienceBiotech
  • Prize: "for their discoveries concerning light-gated ion channels and optogenetics"; 12 million SEK shared equally.
  • Peter Hegemann (Humboldt Univ., Berlin) asked how the alga Chlamydomonas swims towards light; Georg Nagel (Würzburg) discovered channelrhodopsin; Karl Deisseroth (Stanford/HHMI) turned it into a light switch for neurons (2005; in living mice 2007).
  • Method: viral vectors deliver opsin genes → cells express light-sensitive channels → fibre-optic/LED pulses switch neurons ON (ion channels open) or OFF (ion pumps).
  • Uses: mapping circuits in Alzheimer's, addiction, depression; attempts to restore sight.

Chorbagan pandal stampede, Kolkata: toll rises to 19; SIT headed by a sitting High Court judge

GS-III Disaster ManagementCrowd safetyKolkataPrelims + Mains

The death toll in the Saturday stampede at the Chorbagan Sarbajanin Durga Puja pandal (Girish Park area, north Kolkata) climbed to 19 on Sunday – four children and 11 women are among the dead in the front-page report, while the police list of 19 named 13 females, two of them minors (the paper gives both counts as printed).

  • What happened (preliminary reports): heavy evening rain drove visitors into the pandal; the organisers announced that the structure could collapse and asked people to leave; private security staff also blew whistles – adding to the panic. The pandal had a narrow entry and exit (theme: Beyond Sound) and the lanes outside are narrow city gullies.
  • Action: 7 arrested, including the committee's secretary and vice-president; a suo motu case under Section 105 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) – culpable homicide not amounting to murder. Chief Minister Suvendu Adhikari said the Chief Secretary will ask the Calcutta High Court Chief Justice for a Special Investigation Team (SIT) headed by a sitting High Court judge; Kolkata Police officials will be kept out of it because questions are being raised about the police's role.
  • Relief: ₹25 lakh compensation to the next of kin; about 25 injured, 14 shifted to Apollo Hospitals, where Union Home Minister Amit Shah (who cancelled his pandal visits) met them. The Chief Minister (CM) issued crowd-management directions, including restricted access to pandals if needed, and said Bijoya celebrations will be low-key.
  • Editorial – "Beyond religiosity": the State had publicised a 25% rise in pandals to over 57,000; the editorial asks whether safe-management practices were ignored in the drive for spectacle – the CM himself indicated that only temporary home guards had been posted at the pandal.

📌 Static GK

Stampedes are human-induced disasters in India's framework. The Disaster Management Act, 2005 created the NDMA (chair: Prime Minister), SDMAs (chair: Chief Minister) and DDMAs (chair: District Collector). Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023 replaced the Indian Penal Code (BNS s.105 ≈ old IPC s.304 Part II). Durga Puja in Kolkata is on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity (2021).

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • BNS section 105 – culpable homicide not amounting to murder (replaces IPC s.304).
  • NDMA – Disaster Management Act, 2005; Prime Minister (PM) is chair; SDMA – CM; DDMA – Collector.
  • SIT headed by a sitting High Court judge – requires the Chief Justice of the High Court to nominate a judge.
  • Durga Puja, Kolkata – UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage (2021).

UPSC 📝 Mains (GS)

Q. Stampedes at religious and cultural gatherings recur despite guidelines. Examine the institutional and behavioural reasons and suggest measures. (250 words)

Answer skeleton: Intro – Chorbagan (19 dead) and Gwalior (15 dead) in one weekend. Body – narrow ingress/egress, event permissions, panic triggered by unscripted announcements, untrained temporary staff, weather contingency absent, no crowd-density monitoring. Way forward – capacity-based permits, a single trained "voice of calm" announcer, trained police not home guards, weather and drill protocols, organiser accountability, independent audit. Conclusion – festivity must not outrun safety planning.

Motor Vehicles (Amendment) Act, 2019 raised penalties and introduced the Motor Vehicle Accident Fund. A magisterial inquiry is an executive fact-finding inquiry; a judicial inquiry is headed by a judge; the Commissions of Inquiry Act, 1952 governs statutory commissions. PMNRF was set up in 1948 and is funded by public contributions.

Supreme Court bars mid-course fee hikes in professional courses

GS-II EducationJudiciaryFee regulationPrelims + Mains

A Bench of Justices P.S. Narasimha and Alok Aradhe ruled that students entering a four- or five-year professional programme (medicine, engineering) must know their total financial liability at the threshold; mid-stream hikes are impermissible except under unforeseeable or extraordinary economic conditions or statutory changes.

  • Fee-fixation committees must function as continuing institutions – anticipating costs, assessing periodically and deciding on time; authorities must notify the fee for the entire course before admission.
  • Balance: the court is not against periodic revision – unrecovered costs deferred to future batches would cause a revenue deficit and affect quality. The AICTE Regulations, 1994 and the National Education Policy, 2020 already offer frameworks that pre-empt arbitrary hikes by self-financing institutions.
  • Data: citing National Sample Survey Office (NSSO) figures, professional-course fees are 711% higher than for general academic programmes.
🎬 The fee gap
Index is illustrative: general courses = 100; "711% higher" gives about 811.

📌 Static GK

T.M.A. Pai Foundation (2002), Islamic Academy (2003) and P.A. Inamdar (2005) shaped the law on private professional colleges – no capitation fee, committees to fix fees. AICTE (1945; statutory body since 1987) regulates technical education. Right to education flows from Art 21 and Art 21A (6–14 years).

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • T.M.A. Pai (2002), P.A. Inamdar (2005) – private unaided institutions: autonomy but no capitation fee/profiteering.
  • AICTE – statutory body (AICTE Act, 1987) for technical education.
  • NEP 2020 – regulatory reform; single regulator (HECI) proposed.

UPSC 📝 Mains (GS)

Q. Affordability of professional education is as important as access. Discuss with reference to recent judicial directions. (150 words)

Answer skeleton: Intro – Supreme Court (SC) bars mid-term hikes. Body – certainty at admission, continuing fee committees, NSSO 711% gap, loans and family savings, institutional viability; precedents T.M.A. Pai/Inamdar. Way forward – whole-course fee notification, periodic scientific costing, audit. Conclusion – predictability protects both students and institutions.

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

  • Telangana: TAFRC and A.P.: APHERMC fix fees for professional colleges – know the State fee-regulation bodies.

Children and the Internet: ICSSR-backed study and The Hindu's editorial on developing minds

GS-II Social IssuesGS-III TechnologyDigital childhoodMains

A study supported by the Indian Council of Social Science Research (ICSSR), titled Empowering Children in the Digital Age, interviewed over 2,000 children (9–14 years) in Delhi schools and about 500 parents in 2025; the faculty member who conducted it is from Bennett University.

  • Findings: 97.6% access the Internet at home (smartphones the main device); most spend about 4.5 hours a day; studying is the main purpose, then games, videos and chatting. About 47% talked to unknown people in the past six months; strangers, games and age-inappropriate content were more common at 12–13 years. Many struggle to stop using it – "signs of addiction" – and most do not report bad experiences to parents or teachers. Anxiety, sleep disturbances and mood changes are the most reported effects.
  • Gender: boys generally use the Internet more (more exposure to harmful content); girls' restricted access can limit digital literacy and self-protection skills.
  • Recommendation: a "collaborative multi-stakeholder framework" and digital literacy for children and parents with active support from schools.
  • Opinion (Derick H. Lindquist): India has about 65 crore people under 25; the brain's prefrontal cortex matures into early adulthood; Artificial Intelligence (AI) can supplant mental effort (not merely offload it). He urges device-free times, "productive struggle" in schools (oral exams, supervised writing) and stronger evidence and accountability from platforms.
🎬 Study snapshot
Study of children aged 9–14 in Delhi schools; the 47% figure is children who talked to unknown people.

📌 Static GK

Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 requires verifiable parental consent to process a child's (under 18) data. Protection of Children from Sexual Offences (POCSO) Act, 2012 covers online child sexual abuse. ICSSR (1969) is an autonomous body under the Ministry of Education that funds social science research. Neuroplasticity – the brain's capacity to adapt with experience.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • DPDP Act, 2023 – verifiable parental consent for data of children (under 18).
  • ICSSR – established 1969; Ministry of Education.
  • Supreme Court has recognised internet access as integral to Art 19(1)(a) (Anuradha Bhasin, 2020).

UPSC 📝 Mains (GS)

Q. Excessive screen exposure is reshaping childhood. Suggest a balanced policy approach that protects children without denying digital opportunity. (250 words)

Answer skeleton: Intro – 97.6% home access, ~4.5 h/day. Body – benefits (learning, community) vs risks (addiction, strangers, cyberbullying, AI replacing effort); gender gaps; platform design. Way forward – digital literacy, device-free times, school policies, child-safe design, DPDP enforcement, independent research. Conclusion – guide, do not ban.

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

CISF to restructure its supervisory sectors from 1 November

GS-III Internal SecurityCISFPrelims

The Central Industrial Security Force (CISF) will overhaul its administrative and operational structure from 1 November, with the approval of the Ministry of Home Affairs, to move from a geography-based to a sector-specific (functional) supervisory structure.

  • New sectors: Government Building, Museums & Monuments and Mass Rapid Transit System (GBMM & MRTS) – New Delhi; Power-I and Power-II; Coal (Eastern Sector, Ranchi); Steel (Central Sector, Bhilai); Petroleum & Natural Gas (Guwahati); West Coast Port and East Coast Port sectors. Airport sectors and the Atomic Energy/Space sector continue under functional arrangements.
  • Why: to improve oversight, build sector-specific expertise and coordinate with ministries and installation managements. CISF is designated a Recognised Security Organisation (RSO) for port facilities under the ISPS Code; it also guards high-security prisons in Jammu and Kashmir and is the nodal agency for aerial security of critical infrastructure.

📌 Static GK

CISF was raised in 1969 (CISF Act, 1968) and is a Central Armed Police Force under the Ministry of Home Affairs; it protects airports, seaports, atomic and space installations, power plants, coal mines, currency note presses, the Delhi Metro and other mass-transit systems. ISPS – International Ship and Port Facility Security Code (International Maritime Organization).

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • CISF – CISF Act, 1968; MHA; guards PSUs, airports, Delhi Metro, atomic/space sites.
  • ISPS Code – IMO code for ship and port security; CISF = Recognised Security Organisation.
  • Central Armed Police Forces: BSF, Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF), CISF, ITBP, SSB (and Assam Rifles).

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

Opinion: delimitation without parliamentary reform will leave MPs "muted"

GS-II PolityParliamentDelimitationMains

Two policy researchers argue that the Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill and the Delimitation Bill, 2026 – which, as printed, raise the Lok Sabha's strength "from 550 to 850" (the paper's figure) – ignore Parliament's limited floor time.

  • Numbers: the 2024 Atlas shows 97.98 crore voters across 543 constituencies (~18 lakh each); Malkajgiri (Telangana) has 37.8 lakh voters, Lakshadweep only 57,953.
  • Floor time: each sitting allows at most 20 starred and 230 unstarred questions; Question Hour ran for just 60% of scheduled time in the 17th Lok Sabha and only 1% (nine minutes) in the 2026 Monsoon Session (as the authors state).
  • Proposal: give sitting time statutory status – expand Article 85(1) with a minimum working-days clause (the NCRWC recommended 120 days a year) and protect Zero Hour and Question Hour.
🎬 Unequal constituencies
Electors per constituency as cited from the Election Commission of India (ECI) 2024 Atlas.

📌 Static GK

Delimitation Commission – Delimitation Act; its orders have the force of law and cannot be questioned in any court (Art 329). Seats were frozen on the basis of the 1971 Census by the 42nd Amendment (1976) and the freeze was extended to 2026 by the 84th Amendment (2001); Art 81 – Lok Sabha maximum 550 (530 States + 20 UTs). Art 85(1): not more than six months between two sessions. NCRWC (2000–02, Justice Venkatachaliah).

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • Art 81 – Lok Sabha: maximum 550 members; Art 82 – readjustment after each Census.
  • 42nd (1976) and 84th (2001) Amendments – freeze on seats till first Census after 2026.
  • Art 85(1) – six-month rule; Question Hour, Zero Hour (not in the rules; a convention).
  • NCRWC – 120 days of sitting for Lok Sabha.

Way forward – statutory minimum sitting days, protected Question Hour, committee strengthening. Conclusion – representation means a voice, not only a seat.

Money explainer: what if a cheque bounces? Section 138 and the legal clock

GS-III EconomyBanking lawNI ActPrelims

A bounced (dishonoured) cheque can be a criminal offence under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 – imprisonment up to two years, a fine up to twice the cheque amount, or both – if statutory conditions are met.

  • Presumption: under ss.118 and 139 the court presumes the cheque was issued for consideration and towards a legally enforceable debt, unless the drawer proves otherwise (a "probable defence" can rebut it).
  • The clock: present within validity (usually 3 months); written demand notice within 30 days of bank information; drawer has 15 days to pay; complaint within 30 days after that (late complaint accepted for sufficient reason). Section 147 allows compounding (settlement); a civil recovery or summary suit is also possible; limitation for money recovery is generally 3 years.
  • Also in the money pages: "cashless" insurance only removes the need to fund the insurer's share – co-payments, non-admissible items, room-rent limits and sum-insured caps remain with the patient; Sec 80U gives a flat ₹75,000 (₹1.25 lakh for severe disability) deduction under the old regime, as stated in a personal-finance column.

Science: a "Jekyll-and-Hyde" gut protein, RelB, and what it means for IBD treatment

GS-III Science & TechnologyHealthNII DelhiPrelims

A study in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences by researchers at the National Institute of Immunology (NII), New Delhi, led by Soumen Basak, traced the gut's immune balancing act to a protein called RelB.

  • Finding: in mice lacking RelB in these cells, the IL-23-producing dendritic cells in gut immune outposts (SILTs) nearly vanished – RelB builds the defenders, rather than only helping existing cells make IL-23. It works by switching on a second protein, RBP-J, which unlocks a separate communication network.
  • Two faces: in the nearby lymph nodes RelB suppresses regulatory T-cells (the immune system's peacekeepers), worsening chronic intestinal inflammation. One explanation offered: RelB is a weak activator, so where its stronger cousin RelA is present it looks like a repressor, but on genes RelA cannot reach, it activates.
  • Why it matters: drugs blocking IL-23 are frontline treatment for inflammatory bowel disease (IBD); RelB is an "intriguing but delicate" target. Caveat: mouse study; it is unclear whether RelB is needed to build the cell clusters or to keep them alive.

A study in Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society, under a monitoring programme called SNIFFLES, found that low-Earth-orbit (LEO) communications satellites emit radio signals that interfere with astronomy across many frequencies.

  • How: the Mopra telescope and the Australia Telescope Compact Array tracked Starlink, OneWeb and China's Guowang satellites for ~376 hours from April 2024.
  • Findings: a OneWeb signal at 1,613.19 MHz could interfere with telescopes studying hydroxyl (OH) in space; radiation at 2,700 Hz in about 77% of observations from one Starlink version; some signals were 100 billion times brighter than typical astronomical sources.

Pattanam (Muziris): a decade of disruption – the case for reviving research

P.J. Cherian, Director of the Pattanam excavations (2006–2023), argues that research at Pattanam (near North Paravur, Ernakulam, Keralam) must resume.

  • Evidence: from about 300 BCE to 300 CE (most from 100 BCE–300 CE): structures, pottery, beads, metals, coins, workshop and harbour contexts, including a wooden canoe dated 100 BCE–100 CE; materials from South Asia, West Asia, the Red Sea, Africa and the Mediterranean. Pattanam is part of the world conventionally associated with Muziris (Muciri Pattinam), though whether the whole port equals Pattanam is still open.
  • Scale: 67 trenches (2006–2023), ~1.3 lakh registered artefacts, ~45 lakh pottery pieces – yet under 1% of a 100+ acre mound excavated. Finds include a head identified as the Roman emperor Domitian and a sphinx-like ring-stone.
  • Gap: the programme was disrupted in 2016; the PAMA Institute (2018) kept research alive with ASI permissions for 2019–20 and 2021–22 and trenches 62–67 (2020–23). The author proposes an autonomous Pattanam Heritage Authority and a "Viswa Bharathi of the 21st century".

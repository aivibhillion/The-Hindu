Election Commission announces Special Summary Revision in 17 States/UTs; auto-generated Form 7s questioned

GS-II PolityElectoral rollsSSR · SIR · Form 7Prelims hot

Amid protests against the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) and Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) Gyanesh Kumar, the Election Commission of India (ECI) announced a Special Summary Revision (SSR) of electoral rolls in 17 States and Union Territories, including poll-bound Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh and Punjab. The annual exercise was stalled last year because of the Special Intensive Revision (SIR).

  • Which States: Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Assam, Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Goa, Gujarat, Kerala, Lakshadweep, Madhya Pradesh, Manipur, Puducherry, Punjab, Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu, Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand and West Bengal. Where the SIR is going on or just finished (16 States/UTs, including A.P., Telangana, Karnataka, Maharashtra and Delhi) the SSR schedule will come later; for Himachal Pradesh, Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh it will follow weather considerations.
  • Dates: qualifying date 1 January 2027, with advance applications for the later qualifying dates of 1 April, 1 July and 1 October 2027.
  • How it differs from the SIR: no documents to prove citizenship and no mapping to earlier rolls; no house-to-house visits by booth-level officers (draft and final lists go up at polling booths). Firsts: a "readable People's Defence Forces (PDF)" of the alphabetical rolls for candidates and recognised parties, and supplementary voter lists shared with parties.
  • Alongside: since 29 September a special re-enrolment drive (camps and house visits) uses the original Form 6 without the SIR declaration; the Election Commission of India (ECI) also capped the number of Form 7 (deletion) applications one person can file. The paper says Election Commissioners S.S. Sandhu and Vivek Joshi are understood to have flagged the SSR as an anomaly (sources).
  • Auto-generated Form 7s: in Delhi, Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand and West Bengal, electoral registration officers (EROs) were named as "objectors" on forms the ECI software ERONet generated when they deleted voters in SIR hearings, with no written ECI protocol; reports say 87,000+ names in U.P. and 95,000+ in Uttarakhand went this way. The Manual on Electoral Rolls, 2024 has no provision for an ERO to file Form 7.
🎬 Where the SSR applies
17 + 16 + 3 = 36 States/UTs (28 States and 8 UTs); the 16 is derived from the list in the order.

📌 Static GK

The Representation of the People Act, 1950 (s.21) provides for preparing and revising electoral rolls; Article 324 gives the ECI superintendence of elections; Article 326 grants adult suffrage. Form 6 – inclusion; Form 7 – objection/deletion; Form 8 – correction; Form 10 – list of Form 7 applications. ERO = Electoral Registration Officer; Booth Level Officer (BLO) = Booth-Level Officer; Baloch Liberation Army (BLA) = Booth-Level Agent (party appointee).

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • SSR – Special Summary Revision, qualifying date 1 Jan 2027, advance claims for 1 Apr/1 Jul/1 Oct 2027.
  • Form 6/7/8/10; ERO / BLO / BLA; ERONet – ECI software.
  • Representation of the People Act (RP Act) 1950 s.21; Art 324/326; the Election Commission has three members.
  • Readable PDF – parties had sought machine-readable (searchable) rolls.

UPSC 📝 Mains (GS)

Q. Compare the Special Intensive Revision and the Special Summary Revision of electoral rolls. Which better protects the right to vote? (250 words)

Answer skeleton: Intro – 13 crore SIR deletions, then an SSR. Body – SIR: document-based, house visits, mapping; SSR: self-declaration, booth-displayed lists, no house visits; deletion safeguards (Form 7 caps, ERO verification); concerns on auto-generated Form 7s and transparency. Way forward – publish deletion lists, written SOPs, machine-readable rolls, quick appeals. Conclusion – accuracy and inclusion must rise together.

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

  • A.P. and Telangana: SIR is on or just done; SSR schedule to follow.
  • Election Commission – constitutional body (Art 324); three members now.

GS-II PolityProtest rightsSection 163 BNSSPrelims + Mains

On Thursday the Supreme Court (Chief Justice Surya Kant and Justice Joymalya Bagchi) directed the Centre to explain the prohibitory orders under Section 163 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023 that led to mass detentions of protesters, journalists and lawyers on 10 October, when people demanded the Chief Election Commissioner (CEC)'s resignation.

  • Court's steps: a general order to preserve all CCTV footage (including from detention centres); notice to the CEC and Election Commissioners Sukhbir Sandhu and Vivek Joshi on a petition by Opposition leaders led by Rahul Gandhi seeking disclosure of Special Intensive Revision (SIR) records and an independent probe; notice on a plea for criminal contempt action against Mr Gandhi and others for leading a "political procession" into the Court on 12 Oct.   • Jantar Mantar: the Home Ministry told the Delhi High Court that it was denotified after the Supreme Court's 9 Oct directive to find a spacious, open alternative; before the Supreme Court, the authorities said the denotification was not based on that order but on expert opinion (both versions as printed). The Court refused interim orders without hearing the Centre.
  • Opinion ("Out of site"): keeping protesters far from Parliament makes them invisible; a central protest site is part of democratic space in cities such as Washington and London.
  • Politics: YSR Congress Party (YSRCP) president Y.S. Jagan Mohan Reddy said the arrests of Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) members Saurav Das and Ashutosh Ranka (five days' judicial custody for waving the Tricolour at Jantar Mantar) "do not solve anything"; the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP)'s Satya Kumar Yadav and P.V.N. Madhav replied that the Opposition blames the process only when it loses (claims of the named leaders).

📌 Static GK

Section 163 Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) replaced Section 144 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973: it lets a District Magistrate or other empowered officer issue orders in urgent cases of nuisance or apprehended danger. Article 19(1)(b) guarantees the right to assemble peacefully and without arms, subject to Article 19(3). In Mazdoor Kisan Shakti Sangathan v. Union of India (2018) the Supreme Court held that protest sites must be reasonable and not a blanket ban at Jantar Mantar.

Explainer: Section 16 of the 2023 law gives the CEC and Election Commissioners lifelong immunity – should it stay?

GS-II PolityElection CommissionSection 16Prelims + Mains

The Supreme Court has issued notice to the Centre on a plea by the Association for Democratic Reforms (ADR) challenging Section 16 of the Chief Election Commissioner and Other Election Commissioners (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Act, 2023, which grants the Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) and Election Commissioners (ECs) lifelong immunity for acts done in official duty.

  • The argument: the clause is wider than the immunities the Constitution gives the President and Governors (Art 361) or the privileges of legislators; the earlier 1991 Act had no such provision.
  • How it arrived: it was not in the original Bill and was added as an amendment on the day the Bill passed the Rajya Sabha; the Lok Sabha cleared it and the President assented.
🎬 Section 16: the path
As in the explainer.

📌 Static GK

Article 324 establishes the Election Commission of India (ECI); the CEC can be removed only like a Supreme Court judge (Article 324(5)). Article 361 gives the President and Governors immunity from court proceedings for official acts during their term. Anoop Baranwal v. Union of India (2023) had set an interim selection panel including the Chief Justice of India; the 2023 Act replaced the CJI with a Union Cabinet Minister.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • Section 16 (2023 Act) – lifelong immunity for CEC/ECs for official acts.
  • Art 324(5) – CEC removal like a Supreme Court judge; ECs on the CEC's recommendation.
  • Art 361 – President/Governor immunity (term-bound).
  • Anoop Baranwal (2023) – appointment panel; 2023 Act selection committee: Prime Minister (PM), a Union Cabinet Minister, LoP.

UPSC 📝 Mains (GS)

Q. Evaluate the case for immunity to election commissioners. Does lifelong immunity weaken accountability? (150 words)

Answer skeleton: Intro – ADR challenge to Section 16. Body – independence needs protection from harassment, but blanket lifelong immunity exceeds constitutional immunities, was added late without debate, and the 1991 Act had none. Way forward – limit to bona fide acts, retain judicial review. Conclusion – independence with accountability.

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

  • ECI – three-member body; CEC Gyanesh Kumar.

Supreme Court: conversion does not automatically bring quota benefits

GS-II PolityReservationArt 341Prelims + Mains

The Supreme Court dismissed Tamil Nadu's appeal seeking confirmation of a March 2024 order giving reservation benefits to Backward Classes, Most Backward Classes, De-notified Communities and Scheduled Castes who embraced Islam. A Bench headed by Justice Prashant Kumar Mishra held that a Backward Hindu does not automatically become a member of a notified Backward Muslim community by converting.

  • The case: Sameer Ahamed of Thoothukudi, born to Hindu parents, converted in 2015 and got a conversion certificate from the Sunnath Jamath, but the Tahsildar refused a "Muslim Lebbai" community certificate; the Madras High Court rejected his petition.
  • Reasoning: membership of a backward community is historically constituted by social membership; "A Jamath may certify that a person has embraced Islam... it cannot... conclusively determine a statutory Backward Class status." Scheduled Caste status is governed by Article 341 (only the President can notify) and Paragraph 3 of the Constitution (Scheduled Castes) Order, 1950: a person professing a religion other than Hindu, Sikh or Buddhist is not an Supreme Court (SC).
🎬 The case in steps
As reported.

📌 Static GK

Article 341 – President specifies SCs (State-wise, after consulting the Governor); Parliament alone can change the list. The 1950 Order originally limited SC status to Hindus; Sikhs were added in 1956 and Buddhists in 1990. Article 15(4) and 16(4) enable reservation for backward classes; Article 340 provides for a Commission to investigate backwardness.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • Art 341 – SC list by Presidential order; Parliament alone amends.
  • SC Order 1950, para 3 – Hindu, Sikh, Buddhist only.
  • Art 15(4), 16(4), 340 – backward-class provisions.
  • Muslim Lebbai – the community certificate sought; the order refers to seven pre-existing Backward Muslim communities.

UPSC 📝 Mains (GS)

Q. Can religion conversion change entitlement to reservation? Discuss the constitutional position. (150 words)

Answer skeleton: Intro – Supreme Court on the Tamil Nadu order. Body – Art 341 and the 1950 Order bar SC status on conversion outside Hindu/Sikh/Buddhist; Other Backward Classes (OBC) status depends on community membership; states cannot rewrite SC lists by executive order. Way forward – a Commission-based approach, clear tests of social membership. Conclusion – the Court ties benefits to community, not certificates.

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

  • Thoothukudi – Tamil Nadu; locate on the map.

GS-I SocietyWomen's rightsArt 38, 39, 46Prelims + Mains

An opinion piece on the National Commission for Women's consultation on women's right to property argues that empowerment should move from welfare and compensatory justice to distributive justice.

  • Constitutional basis: Directive Principles Articles 38, 39 and 46; the Supreme Court in Air India Statutory Corporation v. United Labour Union (1996) and Consumer Education and Research Centre v.   • Property rights: the Hindu Succession Act, 1956 and its 2005 amendment gave daughters equal coparcenary rights; Vineeta Sharma v. Rakesh Sharma (2020) held that a daughter is a coparcener by birth regardless of whether the father was alive in 2005.

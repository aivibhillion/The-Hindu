1. INDIA bloc to launch campaign against CEC and SIR from 2 October

GS-II PolityElectionsConstitutional BodiesPrelims + Mains

33 leaders from 19 parties of the INDIA bloc met in New Delhi (3½ hours) and announced a nationwide agitation against the Election Commission (EC) and the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls, beginning Friday, 2 October.

  • Plan: protest marches at district headquarters, a march to the Election Commission, and a joint appeal to the Chief Justice of India for early hearing of SIR cases and alleged electoral "malpractices".
  • Rahul Gandhi (Leader of the Opposition, Lok Sabha) said CEC Gyanesh Kumar enjoys "lifelong blanket immunity" because Sec 16 of the CEC and Other ECs (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Act, 2023 bars courts from entertaining civil/criminal proceedings for acts done in official duty.
  • Senior advocate Kapil Sibal suggested a legal challenge to the 2023 Act; Abhishek Singhvi cautioned that a loss in higher courts could legitimise the law.
  • Mamata Banerjee backed Rahul Gandhi as the face of the Opposition; Mallikarjun Kharge said the aim was to "save the country", not to decide leadership. The bloc will form a sub-committee for day-to-day decisions and name spokespersons.
  • DMK was a notable absentee (it has refused to share a stage with the Congress after the Tamil Nadu election). BJP's Shivraj Singh Chouhan called the bloc "fragmented".
🎬 Section 16 immunity and the INDIA bloc plan
Section 16 is the immunity clause the Opposition wants to challenge in court.

📌 Static GK

Art 324: ECI superintends elections. CEC/ECs removal: CEC only like an SC judge (Art 324(5)); ECs only on CEC's recommendation. 2023 Act replaced the earlier 1991 law and changed the selection panel (PM, Leader of Opposition, a Union Cabinet Minister). EC meetings: majority prevails; CEC has no veto (former CEC O.P. Rawat).

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • Art 324; Sec 16 of the CEC & ECs Act, 2023 – immunity for acts in official duty.
  • SIR = Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls; Form 6 (new voters) and Form 7 (objection/deletion) – see Belagavi story below.
  • CEC and the two ECs decide by majority; CEC has no casting veto (O.P. Rawat).

UPSC 📝 Mains (GS)

Q. "Immunity for election commissioners protects independence but weakens accountability." Critically examine in light of the CEC and ECs Act, 2023. (250 words)

Answer skeleton: Intro – 2023 Act, Sec 16 and Opposition campaign. Body – for: insulation from vexatious suits (parallel: judges' protection); against: the selection panel with executive majority, blanket nature of immunity, SIR deletions. Way forward – narrow the immunity (no shield for mala fide acts), transparent SIR, independent secretariat. Conclusion – credibility of elections rests on perceived neutrality.

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

  • SIR in A.P.: EC extended dates – final rolls on 16 Oct (earlier 3 Oct); claims & objections extended to 10 Oct. Meghalaya final roll: 26 Oct (earlier 7 Oct).
  • State Election Commission (Arts 243K/243ZA) runs local-body polls; ECI (Art 324) runs Parliament/Assembly polls and the SIR.

2. SIR controversies: Belagavi Form-7s, Bengal tribunal judge, and a former CEC's warning

GS-II PolityElectionsSIR
  • Belagavi (Karnataka): bulk, pre-filled Form-7 applications seeking deletion of Muslim voters' names reached Booth Level Officers at ≥6 polling stations in Belagavi North; ERO Udayakumar Basavantappa Talawar said someone falsely used his name. Claims/objections period ended 23 Sept; later applications are taken up only after the final roll.
  • West Bengal: Justice Indrajit Chatterjee (retd), heading SIR appellate tribunal no. 11, Murshidabad, offered to resign over a 70–80 km commute. In Murshidabad, ~4.55 lakh names deleted for "logical discrepancies"; statewide ~27.1 lakh removed and 22 lakh+ filed appeals.
  • Former CEC O.P. Rawat: SIR made "so many eligible electors lose their vote for the first time since Independence"; ECs Sukhbir Singh Sandhu and Vivek Joshi objected 14 times over a 10-month period; objections covered changes to Form 6 and the ECI-NET declaration on parents/grandparents in the last SIR roll.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • Form 6 – inclusion of name; Form 7 – objection/deletion; Form 8 – correction/shifting. BLO = Booth Level Officer; ERO = Electoral Registration Officer; ECI-NET = ECI's digital platform.
  • Legal base: Representation of the People Act, 1950 (rolls) and Art 326 (adult suffrage).

UPSC 📝 Mains (GS)

Q. Examine whether mass deletion of electors during SIR threatens universal adult franchise. Suggest safeguards. (250 words)

Answer skeleton: Intro – Art 326 and the SIR exercise. Body – "logical discrepancies" deletions, Form-7 misuse, appeals backlog, burden of documents; EC's need for clean rolls. Way forward – physical hearings, notice with reasons, digitised audit, penal action for false Form-7s, time-bound tribunals.

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

  • A.P. also has SIR under way; final roll due 16 Oct. YSRCP had sought inclusion of voters enrolled up to 15 July in rolls for local polls (see 11 Oct edition).

3. Constitution Bench to examine free speech vs legislative privilege (hearing 6 October)

GS-II PolityJudiciaryArt 194 / 19(1)(a)

Journalists Seema Chishti and Ajit Anjum and stand-up comedian Kunal Kamra sought to intervene in a 2003 case in which the Tamil Nadu Assembly passed a resolution to arrest the editorial staff of The Hindu after an editorial criticised then CM J. Jayalalithaa. The Supreme Court stayed any coercive action against the newspaper's senior editors. A seven-judge Constitution Bench will hear the case on 6 October; Kamra faces privilege proceedings in the Maharashtra Assembly over his show Naya Bharat.

  • Art 194 – powers, privileges and immunities of State legislatures and their members; Art 19(1)(a) – freedom of speech.
  • Question: can a House use its privilege powers to override citizens' fundamental right to freedom of speech and expression? Harish Salve argued for the newspaper that privilege cannot choke press freedom.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • Art 105 (Parliament) / Art 194 (State legislatures) – privileges; Constitution Bench = at least five judges (Art 145(3)); here a seven-judge Bench.
  • Privilege vs fundamental rights: unresolved question of whether privilege is subject to Art 19/21.

UPSC 📝 Mains (GS)

Q. "Legislative privileges must yield to fundamental rights." Discuss with reference to the dispute over press freedom and Art 194. (250 words)

Answer skeleton: Intro – 2003 TN Assembly case. Body – text of Art 194, constitutional balance among legislature, judiciary and press; chilling effect; need to codify privileges. Way forward – codification, procedural safeguards, proportionality test.

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

  • A.P. note: the State government constituted a high-power committee (G.O. 1913) to look into attacks, murders and harassment of journalists (reported on page 3).

4. SC rules out total ban on firecrackers; backs "new-generation joined green crackers"

GS-III EnvironmentGS-II JudiciaryPrelims hot
  • Bench led by Justice M.M. Sundresh accepted suggestions by CSIR-NEERI (National Environmental Engineering Research Institute) for limited and conditional relaxation for Deepavali; "total ban will hurt certain traditions", court said, but it will not allow 24-hour bursting; it may fix specific time windows.
  • NEERI norms: noise within 125 dB(AI)/145 dB(C) peak; reduced shell size, number and bursting time; barium-free; ~30% lower emissions than conventional joined crackers; shell shrinkage cut solid waste (4%–32% reduction); duly-licensed makers only.
  • The CPCB and the Union government to continue further barium and decibel tests.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • CSIR-NEERI (Nagpur) – research lab under CSIR; CPCB – statutory body under Water Act, 1974; PESO licenses explosives.
  • Green crackers: lower emission, no barium; noise limit 125 dB(AI) / 145 dB(C) peak.

UPSC 📝 Mains (GS)

Q. Evaluate the Supreme Court's approach of balancing livelihoods, tradition and air quality in the fireworks debate. (150 words)

Answer skeleton: Intro – ruling out total ban. Body – right to health (Art 21) vs livelihood (Art 19(1)(g)), cultural practices, evidence-based regulation (NEERI), enforcement challenges. Way forward – certified products, time windows, monitoring, community-level alternatives.

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

  • Environment Protection Act, 1986 and Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981 – frequent one-liner bases for pollution questions.

A.P. GeographyClimateAPPSC

🎬 SW monsoon 2026 – month-by-month vs the Long Period Average
Season: 759.4 mm vs 868.6 mm normal (–12.6%); lowest since 2015. East & NE India 1,017.1 mm – driest since 1901.

📌 Concept

LPA (Long Period Average) = average of 1971–2020 rainfall; "deficient" when below normal by more than 10%. Northeast monsoon (Oct–Dec) is crucial for Tamil Nadu and south coastal A.P.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

  • A.P. gets SW monsoon rain mostly in Rayalaseema (rain-shadow) and NE monsoon (Oct–Dec) in coastal districts. Forecast: Oct rainfall below normal (probability 33.5–55%); warmer than normal.

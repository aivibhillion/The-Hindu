INDIA bloc "March for Democracy" reaches the Supreme Court; Opposition seeks SIR records

GS-II PolityElectoral rollsSIR · March for DemocracyPrelims + Mains

Opposition parties of the INDIA bloc marched on Monday (12 October) from the Constitution Club on Rafi Marg to the Supreme Court, ending a weekend in which student protesters were detained. Buses carried the leaders; thousands walked alongside, and the Congress claimed 2.5 lakh took part (the party's figure). Police and the Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF) facilitated the procession, and barricades at the Shangri-La roundabout were opened. Prohibitory orders under Section 163 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) were in force.

  • Petition: Rahul Gandhi, Mallikarjun Kharge and other leaders (through senior advocate A.M. Singhvi) asked the Court for "files, agendas, minutes, notings and objections" of Election Commissioners Sukhbir Singh Sandhu and Vivek Joshi on the Special Intensive Revision, an independent audit panel, and an interim suspension of the exercise. The petition says the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) has so far deleted about 13 crore voters. It was filed at 4.37 p.m., after the march.
  • Chief Justice's letter: Chief Justice Surya Kant had declined a meeting but promised that a petition filed by 12 October would be heard "at the earliest possible opportunity"; the Bench agreed to hear three related petitions on 13 October.
  • Rahul Gandhi's press conference: he likened the protests to Lord Shiva's tandav ("anyone can join Shivji ki baraat"), claimed 200 government officers were sharing information with the Opposition, and asked Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar to "turn approver" (the leader's claims; no evidence was offered in the report).
  • Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) response: Union Minister Shivraj Singh Chouhan called the agitation an attempt to create anarchy; Anurag Thakur and Pradeep Bhandari accused Mr Gandhi of invoking religion for votes (statements of the named leaders).

Delhi Police denotify Jantar Mantar; Delhi High Court to hear pleas

GS-II PolityProtest rightsJantar Mantar · Standing OrderPrelims + Mains

Delhi Police on Monday denotified Jantar Mantar as a designated venue for protests and demonstrations. Under a standing order from Police Commissioner Anurag Kumar, three grounds are now designated: Ramlila Maidan (about 4 km away), Burari Ground (about 15 km) and CBD Ground in East Delhi (about 13 km). The order follows a committee set up by the Union Ministry of Home Affairs; revised standard operating procedures will be issued separately.

  • Earlier order: the decision partly modifies Standing Order No. L&O/48/22 (28 April 2022), which confined protests at Jantar Mantar to 10 a.m. to 5 p.m. and did not allow a programme beyond a day.
  • Supreme Court on 9 October: a Bench headed by Chief Justice Surya Kant recorded that all parties were "ad idem" that "suitable alternative sites" with basic amenities must be identified, and urged the Union government to find a "spacious, open" site; central Delhi has no washrooms, medical or drinking-water facilities. The Court recalled its 2018 "golden rule" balancing protest with residents' peace and the Shaheen Bagh (2020) ruling: "The right of the protester has to be balanced with the right of the commuter".
  • Reactions: activists said distance from Parliament, ministries and the press weakens protest; Congress general secretary Jairam Ramesh called it a sign of "extreme panic"; Akhilesh Yadav called it the "biggest defeat" of the government (their statements). The Cockroach Janta Party sought a protest at Jantar Mantar within 24 hours; its founder Abhijeet Dipke alleged "mental torture" after being told of his grandmother's death during preventive detention.
  • Court: the Delhi High Court will hear two petitions on Tuesday on the closure of the site and alleged assault on protesters on 10 October. Delhi Police said about 300 social-media posts alleged to spread rumours were taken down (police sources).

Explained: SIR 2002-04 v. the current SIR

GS-II PolityElectoral reformsText & ContextPrelims + Mains

An explainer by former IAS officer Rangarajan R. compares the current exercise with earlier ones. Section 21(3) of the Representation of the People Act, 1950 lets the Election Commission order a special revision for "any" constituency or part. The Special Intensive Revision (SIR) has been done eight times before, the last in 2002-04. The current SIR began in June 2025 (Bihar), Phase II ran from October 2025 to February 2026 (nine States and three Union Territories) and Phase III from May 2026 (16 States and three Union Territories).

  • 2002 rules: door-to-door verification from a preliminary list; supervisors rechecked at least 25% of households and officers 12%, 5% and 2% (Assistant Electoral Registration Officers, Electoral Registration Officers, District Election Officers); shifted electors were treated as a "transfer", not a deletion; bulk claims and objections were to be "summarily rejected".
  • Current issues: electors not found were left out of the draft; "shifted" electors were not enrolled at the new address; Bihar mandated only 10% random verification; electors must trace themselves or a parent to the older roll; software "logical discrepancies" (spelling differences, more than six voters per ancestor, parent-child age gaps outside 15 to 45 years) removed lakhs; bulk Form 7 filing (the Election Commission capped objections per person only on 9 October).
  • Numbers: about 13.3 crore deleted over three phases: about 2.7 crore (20%) for death, 1.01 crore (about 8%) duplicates and 9.5 crore (71%) "absent, shifted and others".
  • Way forward (author): audit the finalised rolls; door-to-door enumeration; finalise rolls only after all claims and objections; under the proviso to Section 21(3) pre-SIR rolls can stay in force for coming State elections.

"A backlog can be covered only when there is excess water", the Bench said, noting that Karnataka itself faced scarcity. Tamil Nadu says the flow at Billigundulu, the inter-State measuring point, fell short by 11.719 thousand million cubic feet (tmc ft) up to 6 October, and that Karnataka's combined storage of about 49 tmc ft is adequate.

A Bench of Justices Vikram Nath and Sandeep Mehta said the step was commendable but inadequate: "This is just the tip of the iceberg... What about other drugs".

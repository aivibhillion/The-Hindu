Delhi turns into a fortress as police refuse permission for the Cockroach Janta Party protest

GS-II PolityProtest · PolicingJantar Mantar · internet shutdownPrelims + Mains

Delhi Police refused permission for the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) protest planned for Saturday 10 October at Jantar Mantar, the designated protest site. The party had issued a “Final Call To Save Democracy” poster for 10 a.m. Joint Commissioner of Police (New Delhi Range) Nupur Prasad said the organisers had not complied with undertakings given during a previous protest, and that the request, made by letter on 7 October, was short of the usual 10 days’ notice.

  • Ground curbs: dedicated detention centres in all 15 police districts and over 30 blocking points in the New Delhi district; markets and Metro stations shut. Police said a district-wise strategy aims to keep protesters out of central Delhi.
  • Trains: the Railways cancelled 245 trains to or from Delhi (75 Mail/Express including Rajdhani, Tejas and Shatabdi, and 170 passenger trains), citing expected road congestion and crowds around stations. The Railway Ministry said over 5,000 passengers missed trains on 8 October because of protest-related blockades; cancelled tickets get a 100% refund.
  • More forces: about 6,000 more personnel (40 Indo-Tibetan Border Police and 20 Sashastra Seema Bal companies) on top of 7,000 Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF), about 13,000 additional in all, as reported. Union Home Secretary Govind Mohan’s letter told States and Union Territories to stop movement towards Delhi.
  • Digital curbs: a 24-hour internet shutdown in a four-kilometre radius of the Janpath-Kartavya Path crossing from 10 p.m. on Friday (Special Cell notification). The Information Technology Ministry asked social media platforms to limit the reach of content “likely to cause public disorder” and to give priority to complaints from government agencies.
  • Claims of pre-emptive visits: civil-society members and political workers in Delhi, Madhya Pradesh, Goa, Andhra Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh said police visited them and asked them not to join (their claims). In Bijnor, police camped at the home of a Students’ Federation of India leader.
  • Voices: Union Minister Chirag Paswan backed the youth protest (“Nothing can stop them”) and called for dialogue, adding that the Opposition was politicising it. Congress president Mallikarjun Kharge quoted Sholay (“jo dar gaya samjho mar gaya”) and Left parties demanded an end to intimidation.

Supreme Court calls the blanket ban on Delhi’s Metro and rail “over-reaching”

GS-II PolityJudiciary · Fundamental rightsProhibition vs regulationPrelims + Mains

A three-judge Bench headed by Chief Justice of India Surya Kant on Friday heard an impromptu oral mentioning and said the blanket ban on public transport into and within Delhi was “over-reaching”. It distinguished “prohibition” from “regulation” of Metro and rail services. A writ petition by Communist Party of India (Marxist) general secretary M.A. Baby said express trains to Delhi, from as far as Keralam, were cancelled from 2 p.m. on Friday and that travellers were intercepted and detained in several States.

  • Arguments: senior advocate Gopal Sankaranarayanan: “You can’t shut down an entire city because you cannot handle a bunch of protesters.” Advocates pointed out that 57 Metro stations were shut. Kapil Sibal said coercive measures cannot be taken before anything illegal has been done.
  • The Bench’s caution: Justice Joymalya Bagchi said freedom of association is a fundamental right but it must be verified whether permission was obtained; if not, the protest may be illegal. Mr Sankaranarayanan replied that peaceful protests without permission are not illegal unless an offence is committed.
  • Order: Solicitor General Tushar Mehta assured he would impress upon the authorities to reassess; the four-page order asks for a suitable and regulated operation of services. The court also suggested the government find an open, spacious site for lawful protest, since central Delhi has no washrooms, medical or drinking water facilities; the government welcomed the idea.

A writ under Article 32 lets a citizen approach the Supreme Court directly for enforcement of fundamental rights.

Election Commission caps Form 7 deletion requests as bulk filings spread

GS-II PolityElections · Electoral rollsForm 7 · bulk applicationsPrelims

The Election Commission of India (ECI) on Friday capped the number of Form 7 applications (objections seeking deletion of a name) a voter can file. During the period of claims and objections the cap is 10 per individual elector; during continuous updating of rolls it is five per month. The limits apply to both online and physical forms, which until now had no limit for physical filings.

  • Why: The Hindu reported bulk Form 7 filings in the name of Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) leaders from Rajasthan, Gujarat, West Bengal and Karnataka. The Election Commission of India (ECI) said bulk applications on unsubstantiated grounds inconvenience the elector objected to and add to the workload of field staff.
  • Party agents: booth-level agents of recognised parties may file in bulk but not more than 50 forms a day before the draft roll and 10 a day after it.
  • Safeguards: the Manual on Electoral Rolls, 2023 says bulk claims by one person should not be entertained and requires personal cross-verification by the Electoral Registration Officer where one person is the objector in more than five cases. The Registration of Electors Rules, 1960 says late or irregular objections shall be rejected; false or fabricated complaints invite action against the applicant.

House panel asks the National Testing Agency to hold NEET-UG as a computer-based test

GS-II GovernanceEducation · ExamsCBT · NTA · question banksPrelims + Mains

The Parliamentary Standing Committee on Education, which is examining reform of the National Testing Agency (NTA), tabled its report in the Rajya Sabha on Friday. It recommended moving flagship entrance examinations, specifically NEET-UG, to a Computer-Based Test (CBT) mode, strict logistical precautions and a central national registry to bar compromised vendors.

  • Question banks: the panel said the most viable long-term defence against recurring leaks is large, diverse and continuously updated question banks that generate randomised question sets; papers should be sent by hybrid encrypted delivery right up to the start.
  • Scale: multi-session CBT across independent shifts, with normalisation that is “independently audited, mathematically robust, and clearly explained” before implementation. For connectivity gaps it suggested using mid-sized IT labs (100-200 nodes) in public universities, government engineering colleges, polytechnics and accredited schools.
  • People and process: content to be curated mainly by institutions such as IITs, AIIMS and IIMs; strict background checks on paper setters to rule out links with private coaching; no invasive physical searches. The agency has a vacancy rate of over 40% in key permanent posts.
  • Vendors: a centralised inter-agency digital registry of blacklisted vendors, since vendors penalised by one body often win contracts with others.

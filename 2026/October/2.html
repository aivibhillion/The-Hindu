1. UPSC turns 100: PM Modi says future civil servants must tackle deepfakes, algorithmic bias and climate justice

GS-II GovernanceConstitutional BodiesUPSC CentenaryPrelims + Mains

Addressing the centenary celebrations of the Union Public Service Commission (Wednesday), Prime Minister (PM) Narendra Modi said future civil servants would have to navigate "entirely new battlegrounds" – information warfare, deepfakes, algorithmic bias and climate justice.

  • He asked recruitment agencies to adapt by prioritising candidates' foresight and complex problem-solving: "in the selection process, we will also have to give priority to testing complex problem-solving and foresight".
  • Separately, President Droupadi Murmu and the PM hailed flydubai captain Smit Machchhar (see International).
🎬 100 years of the Union Public Service Commission (UPSC)
Dates from static knowledge: the first Public Service Commission began on 1 Oct 1926 after the Lee Commission (1924).

📌 Static GK – UPSC

Arts 315–323 (Part XIV, Chapter II). Members appointed by the President (Art 316); term 6 years or age 65, whichever is earlier; removal by the President only after a Supreme Court inquiry on misbehaviour (Art 317). Annual report goes to the President who lays it before Parliament (Art 323). Art 320 – functions: conduct exams for All-India and Central services. All-India Services are created under Art 312.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • UPSC is a constitutional body (Arts 315–323); the State PSCs (e.g. Andhra Pradesh Public Service Commission (APPSC), TGPSC) are under the same chapter.
  • Removal: President on Supreme Court (SC) inquiry (Art 317) – unlike Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) (Art 324(5)).
  • Origin: Lee Commission (1924) → Public Service Commission (1926) → Federal PSC (GoI Act 1935) → UPSC (1950).

UPSC 📝 Mains (GS)

Way forward – foresight/problem-solving tests, periodic upskilling, protect meritocracy. Conclusion – continuity of values with change of skills.

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

  • A.P. notes: APPSC is conducting the written examination for university faculty posts in December (see State).

2. Bulk Form 7 filings: EC orders a probe after Shivakumar's protest; Karnataka registers five FIRs

GS-II PolityElectionsSIR · Form 7Prelims hot

The Election Commission (EC) ordered a probe into the filing of Form 7 (objection/deletion) applications in bulk, after Karnataka Chief Minister D.K. Shivakumar and his Cabinet colleagues staged a five-hour protest outside the Chief Electoral Officer's office in Bengaluru. He withdrew the protest after the announcement.

  • Chief Executive Officer (CEO) V. Anbu Kumar directed all Electoral Registration Officers (EROs) to act against applicants who "knowingly" make false statements or declarations in Forms 6, 6A, 7 and 8. Such action must be preceded by an inquiry under Rule 20 of the Registration of Electors Rules, 1960.
  • The Karnataka Police registered five First Information Reports (FIRs) over bulk Form 7s invoking the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 – not the Representation of the People Act, because under the latter only Election Commission (EC) officials can file complaints.
  • In Belagavi and Vijayapura, Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) workers who filed Form 7s in bulk withdrew them after being warned of punitive action, saying they had made a mistake. The BJP backed its workers: flagging suspected voters "is not a crime".
  • Parley (page 7): former Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) Ashok Lavasa and petitioner Prasenjit Bose say Section 31 of the Representation of the People Act (RP Act) (false declarations) "has hardly been used against anyone"; in Karnataka's Babaleshwar, 1,363 of the State's 4,000+ Form 7s came from just 10 electors. In West Bengal, ~90% of Form 7s were accepted (5,72,000 of 6,39,000) against a ~21% rejection of Form 6s (7.26 lakh of 34 lakh).
  • Lavasa's suggestions: a separate National Crime Records Bureau (NCRB) data set on election-related offences, a strong monitoring mechanism, and extra scrutiny where a booth sees more than 2% additions/deletions. Bose wants the 1950s-era law updated for the digital age.
🎬 How a Form 7 should be handled
Under the Rules, no decision can be taken on the ERO's own; a formal inquiry is mandatory.

📌 Static GK – electoral rolls

Form 6 – new voter; 6A – overseas elector; Form 7 – objection / deletion; Form 8 – correction, shifting, replacement of Electors Photo Identity Card (EPIC). RP Act, 1950 governs rolls (Sec 31: false declaration – imprisonment up to one year, or fine, or both). RP Act, 1951 governs conduct of elections. Art 326: adult suffrage (18+).

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • Form 7 = objection/deletion; Rule 20 of the Registration of Electors Rules, 1960 requires a formal inquiry.
  • Sec 31, RP Act 1950 punishes false declarations; only EC officials can complain under this Act – hence FIRs under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS).
  • Booth Level Officer (BLO) (booth level officer), ERO (electoral registration officer), CEO (State chief electoral officer) hierarchy.

UPSC 📝 Mains (GS)

Q. Mass deletion of electors through bulk objections threatens free and fair elections. Evaluate the safeguards in the electoral-roll revision process and suggest reforms. (250 words)

Answer skeleton: Intro – Karnataka Form 7 episode; Art 326. Body – misuse by organised filing, burden on the ordinary voter, weak deterrence of Sec 31, lack of data on offences; EC's need for clean rolls. Way forward – penal action, digital audit trail, public display of objectors, NCRB data set, updating the 1950s statutes. Conclusion – inclusion first: no name deleted without notice and hearing.

3. Bombay HC (Goa): a draft roll cannot be the basis for deleting names without a hearing

GS-II PolityElectionsJudiciarySIR

The Bombay High Court bench at Goa (Justices Valmiki Menezes and Amit S. Jamsandekar) said the primary mandate of the Election Commission (EC) is to ensure that citizens cast their votes, and that the draft roll under the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) cannot be the basis for deletion of names from the voter list.

  • Petition by six members of a family from Santa Cruz constituency, whose names were deleted after they filed Form 8 for a change of address. The ERONET software marked their form "reject" because no Electors Photo Identity Card (EPIC) record was found.
  • Court: "Show us from the Act… what empowers you to delete without a hearing?" The Bench questioned automated deletions and said Forms 6, 7 and 8 "have no meaning" otherwise; the EC said "the software system did not allow" it. Next hearing: 5 October.
  • Rahul Gandhi alleged the online Form 6 was changed "illegally" and asked who ordered Tata Consultancy Services (TCS) to do it; Jairam Ramesh cited an interview of former Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) O.P. Rawat. The EC integrated ERONET into the umbrella platform ECINet in January 2026; ERONET 2.0 is built by Tata Consultancy Services (TCS) (earlier managed by C-DAC).

📌 Static GK

ECINet is the EC's umbrella digital platform (ERONET was integrated into it in Jan 2026, as reported). EPIC = Electors Photo Identity Card (introduced by T.N. Seshan era, 1993). Centre for Development of Advanced Computing (C-DAC) works under Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY).

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • ECINet / ERONET – digital platform for roll management; ERONET 2.0 built by TCS.
  • Principle stressed: audi alteram partem (hear the other side) – no deletion without notice and hearing.
  • Art 324 gives EC superintendence, direction and control of rolls.

UPSC 📝 Mains (GS)

Q. Examine whether automated systems can be used to delete names from electoral rolls consistent with principles of natural justice. (150 words)

Answer skeleton: Intro – Bombay High Court (HC) (Goa) order. Body – draft roll ≠ final roll; automated "reject" without hearing; Form 6/7/8 meaningless; EC's need for efficiency and clean rolls. Way forward – human review, notice and hearing, audit logs, appeal. Conclusion – technology must serve inclusion.

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

  • A.P. needs both ECINet and Booth Level Officer (BLO) verification during SIR; A.P. draft roll had 3,71,38,182 voters.

7. SC: no licence renewal, registration or transfer for motorists with unpaid e-challans

GS-II GovernanceTransportJudiciaryPrelims hot

A Bench of Justices J.B. Pardiwala and K.V. Viswanathan (order of 28 September, made public Thursday) directed all States and UTs to block online and vehicle-related services for motorists with outstanding traffic fines – registration renewals, fitness and Pollution Under Control (PUC) certificates, ownership transfers and licence renewals.

  • Vehicles with unpaid e-challans are to be blacklisted on the Central Parivahan portal, effectively barring sale or transfer until dues are cleared; impounding after repeated violations; withholding of driving-licence renewal and suspension of existing licences in multiple-violation cases.
  • States must identify locations for electronic enforcement (CCTV, speed cameras, speed guns), complete procurement and deployment, and implement the Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) of the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways (28 Oct 2025) under Section 136A of the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 read with Rule 167A of the Central Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989.
  • The court was told about ₹49,149.05 crore of e-challans outstanding nationwide against ₹26,175.05 crore recovered. It also asked for random checks on the ground.
  • The case is a road-safety matter the Supreme Court (SC) has been monitoring for ~14 years (since 2012). It also sought the steps Delhi has taken on its 15 September directions on pedestrian safety along Mathura Road near the Delhi High Court and the National Zoological Park (amicus curiae Gaurav Agrawal).
🎬 Fines outstanding vs recovered
The court says technology-enabled enforcement is useless unless penalties are actually recovered.
🎬 What happens to a defaulter
Services blocked until dues are cleared (Parivahan portal – VAHAN/SARATHI).

📌 Static GK

MV (Amendment) Act, 2019 inserted Sec 136A (electronic monitoring and enforcement) and raised penalties. Parivahan (VAHAN – vehicles; SARATHI – licences) is MoRTH's e-governance platform. Motor vehicles are in the Concurrent List (Entry 35, List III). Road safety – 4 Es: engineering, enforcement, education, emergency care.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • Section 136A, MV Act 1988 and Rule 167A CMVR 1989 – electronic monitoring/enforcement of road safety.
  • Central Parivahan portal – VAHAN (vehicle registry) and SARATHI (licences).
  • Unpaid e-challans: ₹49,149 crore outstanding vs ₹26,175 crore recovered.

1. A.P. High Court sets aside 34% BC quota in local-body polls, citing the 50% ceiling

A.P. PolityGS-II ReservationLocal bodiesAPPSC high-yield

A Division Bench of the A.P. High Court (Chief Justice Lisa Gill and Justice Challa Gunaranjan) set aside G.O. Nos. 105 and 1065, which provided 34% reservation for Backward Classes in Panchayat Raj Institutions and 33.33% in Urban Local Bodies, saying they exceeded the 50% threshold laid down by the Supreme Court.

  • The Bench gave the government liberty to revisit the issue and determine the percentage of BC reservation in view of Janhit Abhiyan vs. Union of India after completing the exercise of data collection as required by Vikas Kishanrao Gawali vs. State of Maharashtra. It held that the State's unified family survey data cannot form the basis of BC quotas.
  • Elections to Panchayat Raj Institutions (PRIs) and Urban Local Bodies (ULBs) must be held within six weeks of 1 October; the terms of PRIs and ULBs expired on 2 April 2026 and 17 March 2026. The Special Intensive Revision (SIR) cannot be a reason to postpone the polls, and Census freezing of administrative boundaries does not bar delimitation of wards in municipalities.
  • The Chief Justice said there was no ground to issue a mandamus directing the State to sub-classify BCs – it is for the State to prove that a group carved out of a larger group is more disadvantageously placed.
  • Chief Minister (CM) N. Chandrababu Naidu said the government will move the Supreme Court and accused the YSR Congress Party (YSRCP) of going to court; the Cabinet had approved the quotas on 18 August. He said the previous government had cut the quota to 24% (16,800 people lost opportunities) and that local bodies stood to lose ₹12,150 crore of Central funds if polls were not held by October. YSRCP chief Y.S. Jagan Mohan Reddy said the government "misled BCs" by issuing orders without a legally sustainable framework.

1. Xenocortication: human brain tissue grown inside a mouse – what Stanford showed

GS-III S&TBiotechnologyEthicsPrelims + Mains

A study in Nature (September) by Sergiu Pașca's group at Stanford transplanted human brain tissue grown from stem cells into mice whose cerebral cortex was genetically prevented from forming. The graft grew into the vacant space, produced several human cell types, became electrically active, connected to the mouse nervous system and was associated with measurable behavioural differences.

  • The researchers call the approach xenocortication ("xeno" = from another species). Mice were also bred without a functioning immune system so the tissue was not rejected; grafts made up about 92% of the animal's cortical tissue after three months.
  • Limits: it is not a human brain inside a mouse; tissue stayed immature; the study cannot establish that human neurons caused behaviour. Ethics: larger, more mature grafts or transplants into embryos/primates would need far greater scrutiny.
  • Applications: model developmental brain disorders (oxygen deprivation around birth → cerebral palsy), human-specific neuronal diseases; drug testing; regenerative treatments.

The IMD expects El Niño to strengthen and bring below-normal rain over most of the country in Oct–Dec; the Cabinet lifted wheat Minimum Support Price (MSP) by ₹25/quintal and mustard by ₹413 to nudge diversification.

Opinion: "A reflection on two songs and their nationalisms" – Vande Mataram vs Vaishnava Janato

GS-I HistoryGS-II PolityNational SongPrelims + Mains

Senior advocate Sanjay Hegde contrasts Vande Mataram with Gandhi's favourite hymn Vaishnava Janato (Narsinh Mehta, 15th century) on Gandhi Jayanti.

  • 2026 orders: in January the Union Home Ministry directed that all six stanzas of the National Song be performed at official functions with everyone standing; in July a second order made the six-stanza text the "official version" and placed the Song before the National Anthem. Parliament amended the Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, 1971, bringing the National Song within criminal law for the first time – up to three years' imprisonment for intentionally preventing its singing or disturbing an assembly singing it.
  • Courts: on 22 Sept the Supreme Court heard T.M. Krishna's challenge but did not decide; Justice Joymalya Bagchi said Bijoe Emmanuel (the right to silent objection) still governs. The Solicitor General stressed national consensus.
  • History: Bankim Chandra wrote the first two stanzas (~1875); later stanzas came with Anandamath and tie the motherland to Durga, Lakshmi and Saraswati. The Congress Working Committee (Oct 1937) – on a resolution moved by Rajendra Prasad and seconded by Vallabhbhai Patel – adopted only the first two stanzas. On 24 Jan 1950 Prasad announced Jana Gana Mana as the National Anthem and Vande Mataram "honoured equally" – with no penal sanction.
  • Modest proposal: make Vaishnava Janato the National Prayer, honoured by usage, with no compulsion – "Vande Mataram tests your posture; Vaishnava Janato tests your conduct".

Text & Context (Full Context): "The forgotten villages at the heart of Delhi's urban history"

GS-I HistoryUrbanisationDelhi DehatMains

Shekhar Tokas (Dr B.R. Ambedkar University Delhi) argues that Delhi's villages (dehat) were integral to the city, but colonial surveys, land acquisition and urbanisation transformed their land and communities.

  • 11 Sept 1803: the East India Company defeated the Maratha army at Patparganj on the Yamuna and gained control of Delhi – start of the colonial mapping and appropriation of the rural landscape. Zones: khadar (Yamuna floodplain), pahari (Aravalli tract), dabar (low-lying south-west around the Sahibi) and bangar (north-west).
  • Land classes: abadi deh (settlement), zari/pahari arazi (agricultural/hilly), shamilat deh (commons). Records: shajra naksh (map with khasra numbers), jamabandi, khatauni. Four settlements culminated in 1880; Delhi district was organised into three tehsils, 44 zails and hundreds of villages (375 villages across 18 zails ≈ present-day Delhi).
  • The 1908 Lal Dora system formalised village boundaries. After the capital shift (1911), land acquisition displaced ~30 villages (Raisina, Khairpur – Viceroy's House, Malcha – Chanakyapuri). Today "urban villages" such as Munirka remain inside the planning history; Delhi Dehat deserves heritage-sensitive planning.

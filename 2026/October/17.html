  • Mumbai: a "Lehra Do Tiranga" protest at Azad Maidan demanded the Chief Election Commissioner (CEC)'s resignation, withdrawal of the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) and release of detained activists; a protester said Mumbai Police cooperated.

Election Commission: Special Summary Revision in 17 States/UTs – "a necessary corrective" after the SIR

GS-II PolityElectoral reformsSSR · SIRPrelims hot

The Election Commission of India (ECI) has announced a Special Summary Revision (SSR) of electoral rolls in 17 States and Union Territories, with 1 January 2027 as the qualifying date. The Hindu's editorial, "Necessary corrective", welcomes the changes but demands that Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) Gyanesh Kumar resign; letters to the editor question the timing.

  • What changes: a special re-enrolment drive that does not require the ancestry declaration; a cap on Form 7 (objection) applications; searchable rolls (readers' letters mention text PDFs).
  • Why a corrective (editorial): West Bengal's appellate tribunals disposed of only about 3% of 38 lakh appeals but restored 93% of electors in the decided cases; in Bihar the gender ratio on the roll slid from 907 to 892. The Supreme Court upheld the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) in May, but the editorial says trust in the ECI is eroded.
  • Manipur: its SSR also uses 1 Jan 2027; final rolls on 4 Jan 2027. The SIR there deleted 1,32,456 names, leaving 19,60,620 voters in a State of 60 Assembly seats.
  • Letters: readers ask why the "far more intrusive" SIR was needed, note that "Special" and "Intensive" were conflated although neither the Constitution nor the statute names the procedure, and say even the two Election Commissioners are understood to have raised concerns about the timing (reader claims).

📌 Static GK

Section 21 of the Representation of the People Act, 1950 provides for preparation and revision of electoral rolls, including special revision. The qualifying date is the date on which a person must be 18 to enrol. Article 324 vests superintendence of elections in the ECI; Article 326 gives adult suffrage. Form 6 – inclusion; Form 7 – objection/deletion; Form 8 – correction.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • SSR – Special Summary Revision; SIR – Special Intensive Revision. Qualifying date 1 Jan 2027.
  • Representation of the People Act (RP Act) 1950 s.21 – roll revision; Art 324/326.
  • Form 7 – objection/deletion (capped per the SSR).
  • Manipur – 60 Assembly seats; final roll 4 Jan 2027.

UPSC 📝 Mains (GS)

Q. Electoral rolls are the foundation of free and fair elections. Evaluate the Special Intensive Revision and the Special Summary Revision in this light. (250 words)

Answer skeleton: Intro – the SIR sparked deletion disputes; the SSR is described as a corrective. Body – inclusion versus purification; burden of proof on citizens (ancestry declaration); tribunal backlog (3% of 38 lakh appeals disposed); gender-ratio fall in Bihar; Form 7 abuse; trust deficit. Way forward – transparent deletion lists, fast appeals, door-to-door verification, independent audit. Conclusion – the aim is a complete and accurate roll, not a smaller one.

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

  • Election Commission of India – constitutional body under Art 324.

Delimitation: Kharge asks PM for an all-party meeting; the Monsoon Session is still not prorogued

GS-II PolityConstitutional amendmentsDelimitationPrelims + Mains

Congress president Mallikarjun Kharge wrote to the Prime Minister seeking an all-party meeting on delimitation. The earlier constitutional amendment had failed the two-thirds test in the Lok Sabha on 17 April (362 votes were needed, as printed).

  • Parliament stuck: the Monsoon Session was adjourned sine die on 13 August and has not been prorogued 64 days later (the count is derived from the dates).
  • Why it matters: a session that is adjourned but not prorogued stays technically alive, which affects what the Government can do through ordinances (an ordinance can be issued only when Parliament is not in session).
  • Linked debates: the southern States fear a loss of political weight if seats are redrawn on population; the paper reports the demand for a consultation, not a settlement.
🎬 Delimitation timeline
Dates as printed; the 64-day gap is derived.

📌 Static GK

Articles 81 and 82 deal with the composition of the Lok Sabha and readjustment after each census; Article 368 needs a majority of the total membership and two-thirds of members present and voting for a constitutional amendment. Adjournment sine die is by the presiding officer; prorogation is by the President. The Delimitation Commission is set up under a law of Parliament; its orders have the force of law and cannot be questioned in any court (Art 329).

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • Art 81/82 – seats and readjustment; Art 368 – amendment procedure.
  • Adjournment sine die v. prorogation (President) v. dissolution.
  • Delimitation Commission – orders final; Art 329.
  • Ordinance – Art 123; only when Parliament is not in session.

UPSC 📝 Mains (GS)

Q. Delimitation after the census is linked to federal balance. Discuss the concerns of southern States and suggest a way forward. (250 words)

Answer skeleton: Intro – amendment failed in April; Kharge seeks an all-party meeting. Body – population-based redrawing penalises States that controlled fertility; freeze on seats until the first census after 2026; fiscal federalism parallels; representation of women. Way forward – all-party consensus, a formula that rewards demographic performance, protecting proportional shares. Conclusion – federal trust is the currency of delimitation.

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

  • A.P./Telangana: southern-State concerns on seat share after delimitation.
  • Lok Sabha seats – 543 elected.

Article 239 governs Union Territories; Article 240 lets the President make regulations for some UTs.

The north-east (retreating) monsoon brings most of the rain to Tamil Nadu, coastal A.P. and south Karnataka in October-December, when winds blow from land to sea and pick up moisture over the Bay of Bengal. India Meteorological Department (IMD) calls a district's rainfall "deficient" if it is 20% to 59% below normal and "large deficient" if 60% to 99% below normal.

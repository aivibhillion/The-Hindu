Gandhi Jayanti protests: Opposition, students and the Cockroach Janta Party demand Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar’s resignation

GS-II PolityElections · ProtestSIR · Article 19Mains

On 2 October (Gandhi’s birth anniversary) protests were held across the country against the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls and the Chief Election Commissioner (CEC), Gyanesh Kumar. Governments refused permission in several places and imposed prohibitory orders.

  • Delhi: police detained over 500 people including former Chief Minister Atishi (the editorial says over 700, and adds that the Internet was shut in the Jantar Mantar area and 11 Metro stations closed). Rahul Gandhi said: “Their only crime is that they dared to question vote theft”.
  • Mumbai: police refused permission but 4,000-5,000 people sat outside the gates of Shivaji Park, Dadar, with the Tricolour; speakers included actor Shabana Azmi, singer Vishal Dadlani and comedian Rajiv Nigam. A 75-year-old man who had come to watch died of a heart attack.
  • Elsewhere: Communist Party of India (Marxist) rallies in Keralam; Congress workers stopped by police in Srinagar; an Aam Aadmi Party protest in Jaipur; an Opposition rally in Vijayawada against the Special Intensive Revision (SIR).
  • Cockroach Janta Party (CJP): founder Abhijeet Dipke said that if Kumar goes, “the Prime Minister will also have to go”, and called the Mumbai gathering “just a trailer” for Jantar Mantar (Delhi) on 10 October. The CJP also wants a freeze on SIR, the restoration of the January 2025 voter list and repeal of the 2023 law governing appointment of Election Commissioners.

SIR in numbers: about 13.3 crore deletions, a one-month re-enrolment order and a tribunal queue of 22.21 lakh in West Bengal

GS-II PolityElectoral rolls · Election CommissionForm 6 · Appellate tribunalsPrelims + Mains

Three linked developments on 2 October: the Election Commission (EC) ordered a re-enrolment drive for voters left out of the Special Intensive Revision (SIR); The Hindu analysed the deletion data; and the number of pending West Bengal appeals came out in an Election Commission (EC) affidavit.

  • Scale: the EC letter says about 13.3 crore names have been deleted where SIR is complete or under way; the editorial says “nearly 13.4 crore” across 30 States and Union Territories (about 14% of the earlier electorate). Deletion rose from 8.3% in Bihar to 12.9% in phase 2 and 17% in phase 3.
  • Why deleted (The Hindu analysis): about 2.7 crore (21%) declared dead, about 1.01 crore multiple entries and 9.5 crore absent, shifted or other. The paper says this means 72% of those deleted could be back on the rolls once SIR is completed.
  • The EC order: compare the pre-SIR and current rolls within a month, visit homes and use Form 6 for eligible persons, plus booth-level camps. Former Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) S.Y. Quraishi objected that Form 6 is meant only for new voters.
  • West Bengal: the EC told the Supreme Court 22.21 lakh appeals are pending before the 19 appellate tribunals formed on 21 March (the Supreme Court ordered a separate appellate body on 10 March). The drive excludes these cases, so two parallel routes could exist. Nandigram and Rejinagar vote in by-polls on 6 October with over 6,000 and 12,000 appeals pending.
  • Opposition: Congress’s Jairam Ramesh accused the EC of “lying” about the legality of its ECINet platform (launched 22 January); a lawyers’ group announced an independent panel of former judges to report by December 2026.

Trade deal “not imminent”, says U.S. Trade Representative; Goyal tells the G20 India has no excess capacity

GS-III EconomyTrade · G20 · WTOForced labour · Excess capacityMains

U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said the India-U.S. deal is “in the short strokes” but not “imminent”. Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal spoke at the concluding day of the G20 Trade Ministers’ Meeting in Milwaukee (the U.S. holds the 2026 presidency).

  • Forced labour: in July the Office of the U.S. Trade Representative (USTR) levied extra tariffs on 60 countries, India’s being 10%. Goyal said India’s commitment to ending forced labour is “absolute and unconditional”, citing Article 23, ILO Conventions 29 and 105 and a July amendment to the Foreign Trade Policy banning such imports; border action must rest on “specific and verifiable evidence”.
  • Excess capacity: the USTR is probing 60 countries for “structural excess capacity”, which could bring more tariffs. Goyal said India has none in the sectors identified, that concerns should be addressed through WTO-consistent anti-dumping and countervailing duties, and that developing countries must retain “policy space to industrialise”.
  • Stand: Goyal has said India will not sign until it is clear it will get a comparative advantage over competitors, as agreed in February 2026.

📌 Static GK

Article 23 prohibits trafficking and forced labour. The ILO’s Convention 29 (1930) deals with forced labour and Convention 105 (1957) with its abolition. The G20 was founded in 1999 and its presidency rotates. Anti-dumping and countervailing duties are WTO-permitted remedies.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • Art 23 – forced labour; ILO C29 and C105.
  • BTA – Bilateral Trade Agreement.
  • Remedies consistent with World Trade Organization (WTO): anti-dumping, countervailing, safeguard.

Answer skeleton: Intro – tariffs of 10%, USTR probes. Body – Art 23, ILO conventions, FTP amendment; WTO rules; unilateralism; policy space for developing countries. Way forward – negotiate the interim deal, diversify markets. Conclusion – rules-based trade serves smaller economies.

Manipur: Suspension of Operations pact lapses, ₹10 lakh for kin of 49 missing persons, Shah to visit Senapati

GS-III Internal SecurityNorth-East · InsurgencySoO pact · Kuki-Zo · NagaMains

The Union Home Ministry has not extended the Suspension of Operations (SoO) pact with Kuki-Zo groups a month after the one-year agreement expired on 4 September, amid continuing Naga-Kuki violence.

  • Why in abeyance: government sources say it stays so until all weapons are surrendered; the Kuki-Zo side says camps are inspected regularly and weapons accounted for, and that non-extension “does not mean it has been abrogated”.
  • Violence: over 40 people killed in Ukhrul, Kangpokpi and Kamjong since 7 February, three days after President’s Rule was revoked. 30 more Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF) companies (about 3,000) and two CoBRA battalions (about 2,000) are to be sent. Amit Shah is expected in Senapati on 7 October.
  • Missing persons: the Cabinet approved ₹10 lakh each for survivors of the 49 missing since 3 May 2023; a missing person is presumed dead only after seven years. The Home Minister said the conflicts claimed 306 lives and displaced over 60,000.
  • Politics: Chief Minister is Yunman Khemchand Singh; Deputy Chief Minister Losii Dikho has demanded abrogation of the pact.

Plea in the Supreme Court: only the two-stanza Vande Mataram should be the National Song

GS-II PolityFundamental rights · CultureAnandamath · Article 25Prelims

A petition by B.D. Borkar, represented by jurist Mohan Gopal, calls the six-stanza Vande Mataram a “divisive religious war song” and seeks to quash two government orders (28 January and 9 July). It asks the court to direct that the two-stanza standalone version alone be recognised.

  • Argument: the six-stanza version appears in Bankim Chandra Chatterjee’s novel Anandamath, where ascetics pray to Durga, Saraswati and Lakshmi; the plea says compelling it, especially for schoolchildren, violates freedom of religion.
  • History cited: the standalone two-stanza song was first published in 1881 and was treated as the National Song for decades without being officially declared so.
  • Court: on 22 September the Supreme Court observed orally that an elected government cannot compel conscientious objectors to sing or play any stanza under threat of penal action.

📌 Static GK

On 24 January 1950 the Constituent Assembly’s President, Rajendra Prasad, announced that Jana Gana Mana would be the National Anthem and that Vande Mataram would have equal honour and status as the National Song. Article 25 guarantees freedom of conscience; in Bijoe Emmanuel (1986) the Supreme Court protected students who stood silently during the anthem.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • Art 25 – freedom of conscience and religion.
  • Bijoe Emmanuel (1986) – no compulsion to sing the anthem.
  • Anandamath – Bankim Chandra Chatterjee; song first published 1881.

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

  • National Song v National Anthem: adoption on 24 January 1950.

Monsoon report card: 35.4% deficit and below-normal October rain forecast

  • Intensity: one very-heavy-rain day (Visakhapatnam) and 20 heavy-rain days.

₹23,731-crore GOBARdhan scheme aims at a 10-fold rise in compressed biogas

GS-III EnergyBiogas · Waste to energyCBG · Annadata to UrjadataPrelims

The government formally launched the GOBARdhan scheme (₹23,731 crore) to turn agricultural and organic waste into compressed biogas (CBG), with capital assistance and guaranteed offtake.

  • Targets: a 10-fold increase in CBG output (from 0.4 to 4-6 million standard cubic metres a day); raising natural gas in the energy basket to 15% by 2030 from 6-7%.
  • Support: up to ₹30 crore per project; greenfield ₹1.25 crore per tonne a day; brownfield 50% of that; upgrades of existing biogas plants ₹0.60 crore a tonne a day (ceiling ₹5 crore).
  • Offtake: up to 100% of eligible output at ₹2,110 per million British thermal units (about ₹98 a kg).
  • Benefits claimed: save over ₹40,000 crore of fuel imports, add over ₹75,000 crore to GDP, create over 1.5 lakh jobs, replace about 10 million tonnes of fossil fuel and cut over 40 million tonnes of CO2.

Editorial: “A Court divided by its own architecture” — why a split verdict exposes a structural fault

GS-II PolityJudiciary · Constitution BenchesArticle 145(3) · Anoop BaranwalMains

Suhrith Parthasarathy writes that a two-judge bench’s split on the challenge to the Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Act, 2023 reveals a deeper problem in how the Supreme Court is organised.

  • The split: Justice Dipankar Datta rejected the Union’s plea to refer the matter to five judges; Justice Satish Chandra Sharma accepted it. Both asked the CJI to consider a Constitution Bench.
  • The law: Article 145(3) requires a minimum of five judges for a substantial question of constitutional interpretation; Anoop Baranwal (2023) had read Article 324 to require insulation from exclusive executive control.
  • Structure: in 1950 the Court had six judges; today the sanctioned strength is 38, and it sits mostly in panels of two. The Law Commission’s 229th report (2009) proposed a Constitution Bench in Delhi and regional benches for appeals.
  • Suggestions: a permanent Constitution Bench, with composition by transparent, rule-based procedure rather than the CJI’s discretion as Master of the Roster.
🎬 The Election Commissioners appointment case

📌 Static GK

Article 145(3) sets the five-judge minimum; Article 141 makes the Court’s law binding on all courts; Article 324 establishes the Election Commission. The Master of the Roster doctrine (Ashok Pandey and the 2018 Campaign for Judicial Accountability case) gives the CJI the power to assign benches. Shreya Singhal (2015) struck down Section 66A of the IT Act.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • Art 145(3) – five-judge benches; Art 141 – binding precedent.
  • Anoop Baranwal (2023) – interim appointment committee with the CJI.
  • Law Commission 229th report (2009) – Constitution Bench in Delhi.

Opinion: India’s Model Bilateral Investment Treaty — a decade later

GS-III EconomyInvestment treaties · ISDSModel BIT 2015 · MFNMains

Jyoti Singh writes that India’s revamp of its Model Bilateral Investment Treaty (BIT), nearly a decade after the 2015 model, should draw on what new treaties have taught. The Union Budget 2025-26 had announced the model would be made more investor-friendly; it is reportedly finalised and awaiting Cabinet approval.

  • 2015 model: narrower definition of investment and a requirement to exhaust local remedies for at least five years before arbitration, shaped by the White Industries v. India award (2011).
  • Since then: treaties with the United Arab Emirates (UAE), Uzbekistan and Israel; the UAE and Israel agreements allow three years for local remedies; the India-Israel agreement entered into force in July 2026; the Uzbekistan treaty allows counterclaims against investors.
  • Issues for the new model: whether to include a Most Favoured Nation clause and its scope (Maffezini v. Plama); clear drafting on expropriation and fair and equitable treatment; investor obligations; dispute prevention and the UNCITRAL work on reform of investor-state dispute settlement.

A BIT protects foreign investors of one country in the other. ISDS lets investors sue states in arbitration. UNCITRAL (United Nations Commission on International Trade Law) Working Group III examines ISDS reform. UNCTAD publishes the World Investment Report.

17 killed in stampede at Chore Bagan Durga Puja pandal, Kolkata

GS-III Disaster ManagementCrowd safetyKolkataPrelims + Mains

Seventeen people died and several were injured in a stampede at the Chore Bagan Durga Puja pandal in Kolkata (report continued from page one).

  • Condolences and relief: President Droupadi Murmu expressed "deepest condolences"; Prime Minister (PM) Narendra Modi called it "extremely distressing" and announced ₹2 lakh for the families of the dead and ₹50,000 for the injured.
  • Reaction: Union Home Minister Amit Shah reached Kolkata on Saturday evening and cancelled his Sunday programme of pandal visits; former Chief Minister Mamata Banerjee said she was "deeply shocked". Kolkata Police started helplines (8003455678 and 03322141310).

📌 Static GK

Stampedes are treated as human-induced disasters in India's disaster-management framework. The Disaster Management Act, 2005 created the NDMA (chair: Prime Minister), SDMAs and DDMAs. NDMA has issued guidelines on managing crowds at events and venues of mass gathering. Durga Puja in Kolkata was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity (2021).

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • NDMA – apex body under the Disaster Management Act, 2005; chaired by the Prime Minister. State: SDMA (chair: Chief Minister); District: DDMA (chair: Collector).
  • Stampede = man-made (human-induced) disaster; mitigation: crowd-density limits, one-way flows, trained volunteers.
  • Durga Puja, Kolkata – UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage (2021).

The Hindu data: 1.28 lakh Form 7 deletion requests in Karnataka in the month before the SIR began

GS-II PolityElectionsSIR · Form 7Prelims hot

A data-based report finds that between the announcement of the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) (14 May) and the freezing of the rolls (16 June), Karnataka received over 1.28 lakh Form 7 applications seeking deletion of names – before BLOs began distributing enumeration forms on 30 June.

  • Third parties: 45,878 applications (35.6%) were filed by people other than the elector or election officials. Of these, 2,254 were "bulk applications" – 11 or more each – by just 112 objectors. Basamma (Sindhanur) filed 96 (84 against electors of one booth); Hanamant Calavadi (Bagalkot) filed 62, all against one polling station.
  • Reasons cited: death – 36,439; "absent or permanently shifted" – 6,775.
  • Where: in at least 28 Assembly constituencies there were over 1,000 Form 7s (20 held by the Congress). 28,441 were concentrated in four constituencies – Bagalkot, Belagavi North, Sindhanur and Nippani. District-wise: Belagavi 38,713, Bagalkot 13,889, Raichur 10,454. In the week before freezing alone (10–16 June) 20,671 were filed; 3,624 on 14 May.
  • The puzzle: it is unclear why Form 7s were accepted before the SIR began, when enumeration was meant to identify the same categories (Absent, Shifted, Dead, Duplicate, Others) – at the draft-roll stage 1.07 crore names were excluded. Karnataka Chief Electoral Officer V. Anbu Kumar: "pending forms were processed till June 16, 2026". The Hindu had earlier reported that pre-filled Form 7s targeting Muslim electors were handed to BLOs in Belagavi North.

Supreme Court: children, too, have a fundamental right to access and use the Internet

GS-II PolityChild rightsArt 21 + 19(1)(a)Prelims + Mains

A Bench of Justices J.B. Pardiwala and Vinod K. Chandran held: "the right to access and use the Internet is a fundamental right of every individual under Article 21 read with Article 19(1)(a)… Our children, irrespective of their age, enjoy the same rights."

  • No blanket bans: restrictions drawn from other jurisdictions are "unproven measures of proven disproportionality"; no jurisdiction has shown that children are safer because of them. Children simply move to unregulated, unsafe sites and may hide abuse for fear of reprisal – defeating the object of the Protection of Children from Sexual Offences (POCSO) Act.
  • What the Court favoured: "intelligent restraints", greater emphasis on parental consent, guidance and supervision, a dedicated "child mode" on social-media accounts, age-appropriate safeguards and grievance outlets; duties on intermediaries, law enforcement, the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology and courts. The order is not against age-based restrictions as such.

As reported from the order.

📌 Static GK

Anuradha Bhasin v UoI (2020) – freedom of speech and trade through the Internet is protected by Art 19(1)(a) and 19(1)(g); Internet shutdowns must be proportionate. Faheema Shirin v State of Kerala (2019, Kerala HC) – Internet access is part of Art 21. DPDP Act, 2023 requires verifiable parental consent to process the data of children (under 18). POCSO Act, 2012 protects children from sexual offences.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • Right to Internet = Art 21 + Art 19(1)(a) (this order); earlier: Anuradha Bhasin (2020).
  • POCSO Act, 2012; DPDP Act, 2023 – parental consent for children's data.
  • Doctrine of proportionality in restricting rights.

UPSC 📝 Mains (GS)

Answer skeleton: Intro – SC recognises children's right to the Internet. Body – rights vs safety, proportionality, displacement to unsafe platforms, role of parents and platforms, DPDP Act. Way forward – child mode, age verification with privacy, grievance redress, digital literacy. Conclusion – protect, don't exclude.

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

Explained: why was actor Imran Khan asked to leave India? OCI rights and limits

GS-II PolityCitizenshipOCIPrelims + Mains

Actor Imran Khan, a U.S. citizen and Overseas Citizen of India (OCI) cardholder, was asked by the Foreigners Regional Registration Office (FRRO), Mumbai, to leave India by 28 October after he took part on 10 October in a Mumbai protest against Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) Gyanesh Kumar. The Foreigners Regional Registration Office (FRRO) acted on a complaint by Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) leader Kirit Somaiya, citing Sections 7(2)(c) and 29 of the Immigration and Foreigners Act, 2025. He moved the Bombay High Court on Saturday calling the notice arbitrary, illegal and unconstitutional (Articles 14 and 21); the FRRO has issued a second notice asking why his Overseas Citizen of India (OCI) card should not be cancelled (15 days to respond).

  • OCI scheme: not citizenship. Eligibility (s.7A, Citizenship Act, 1955) covers people who were citizens on 26 Jan 1950 or eligible then, and their descendants; those linked to Pakistan, Bangladesh or other notified countries are ineligible.
  • Privileges: lifelong multiple-entry visa; exempt from FRRO registration; parity with Indians on domestic airfares and entry fees; parity with NRIs on buying property (not agricultural land, farmhouses or plantations); can practise medicine, law, etc. subject to laws; can appear for National Eligibility cum Entrance Test (NEET)/JEE for NRI seats. Not allowed without permission: missionary or tabligh work, mountaineering, journalism.
  • Political rights: none – cannot vote or contest (s.7B); cannot hold constitutional offices; Article 19 applies to citizens only, but political views fall under Article 21 which covers foreigners (Kamil Siedczynski, Calcutta High Court (HC), 2020, decided under the older Foreigners Act, 1946).
  • Cancellation (s.7D): fraud, disaffection towards the Constitution, dealings with an enemy, a sentence of 2+ years within 5 years of registration, or sovereignty/security/public-interest grounds. Remedy: representation to the FRRO/Centre; writ under Art 226.

GS-II HealthRegulationFSSAI · NPPAPrelims

NPPA – under the Department of Pharmaceuticals; fixes ceiling prices of scheduled drugs under the Drugs (Prices Control) Order (DPCO) 2013. Codex Alimentarius – international food standards body (FAO/WHO).

Profile: Robert E. Hall and Michael Woodford – 2026 Economics Nobel – "the economy is made out of the people in it"

GS-III EconomyAwards · MacroeconomicsNobel 2026Prelims

Robert E. Hall and Michael Woodford won the 2026 Sveriges Riksbank Prize in Economic Sciences in Memory of Alfred Nobel for showing how households' expectations and firms' pricing behaviour shape the economy and the effect of government policy.

  • Robert E. Hall (born 1943, Palo Alto; Stanford): in a landmark 1978 paper he showed that if households decide on expected future income, changes in consumption cannot be predicted; when they can be, it is because households live hand-to-mouth – letting policymakers measure that share of the population.
  • Michael Woodford (born 1955, Massachusetts; studied at Chicago, Yale Law and MIT): from the 1990s showed that inflation arises from forward-looking firms with sticky prices; hence central-bank credibility and transparency matter (as in the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) Governor's explanations). He combined this with Hall's demand side into a full framework.
  • Background: Keynesian stabilisation after the 1930s Great Depression; broke down in the 1970s oil shock; Robert Lucas (1995 Nobel) pushed for micro-foundations.

IISER Thiruvananthapuram: a solid that releases hydrogen and methane on mild heating

GS-III Science & TechEnergyHydrogenPrelims

Scientists at Indian Institute of Science Education and Research (IISER) Thiruvananthapuram developed magnesium hydridomethylzincate – a compound of magnesium, zinc, hydrogen and carbon – that releases hydrogen (heated in solution at 100°C) and, as crystals without solvent at 80–110°C, both hydrogen and methane. Published in Science Advances on 7 October; lead: Prof. Ajay Venugopal.

  • Why: hydrogen burns without direct CO₂ but is hard to store and transport (very low density). Blending with methane reduces flashback and detonation risk. A solid that releases both gases could replace compressed gas storage.
  • Findings: methane emerged before hydrogen (successive reactions); each unit of its paired structure can yield two molecules each of hydrogen and methane.
  • Limits: sensitive to moisture; not shown to be reversible (cannot yet be "refilled").

Hoolock gibbon: why roads and rail lines fragment India's only ape's habitat

GS-III EnvironmentBiodiversityHoolock gibbonPrelims

The Hoolock gibbon is India's only ape; found mostly south of the Brahmaputra in Northeast India. Western and eastern hoolock populations have fallen by about 90% in recent decades, mainly due to habitat fragmentation by roads and railway lines.

  • Why vulnerable: adults are ~3 ft tall with 5-ft arms; arboreal (brachiation) and need continuous canopy; clumsy on the ground (easy prey for wild dogs and leopards); mate for life and guard territory – so fragmentation causes genetic isolation and inbreeding.
  • Where: fragmentation began when railways entered Eastern Assam in 1887; one fragment became the Hollongapar Gibbon Sanctuary with about 130 western hoolock gibbons, out of 3,000–5,000 in the Northeast. Similar cases: lion-tailed macaque (Valparai, T.N.), slow loris, three-toed sloth.

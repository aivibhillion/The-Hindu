U.S. court lets Pannun serve his civil suit on Ajit Doval and a former RAW chief through Indian channels

GS-II International RelationsIndia–U.S. · Extraterritorial lawJudicial channels · Letters rogatory · Civil suitMains

Judge Katherine Polk Failla of a U.S. court allowed Sikh separatist Gurpatwant Singh Pannun to serve his civil complaint on National Security Adviser Ajit Doval and former Research and Analysis Wing (RAW) chief Samant Goel through Indian judicial channels. The U.S. State Department had served the Indian Embassy (certificate dated 9 October 2026). Pannun must prepare the documents by 30 November. India’s External Affairs Ministry, through spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal, called the suit “frivolous” and said any papers must go through the Ministry of Law and Justice. The suit relates to an alleged June 2023 plot that U.S. authorities say they foiled; these are allegations and India denies wrongdoing by its officials.

Sonia Gandhi’s memoir: remarks on Pranab Mukherjee draw reactions

Delhi: 796 deletion applications by BJP-appointed agents concentrated in 25 booths of Mustafabad

GS-II PolityElectoral rolls · Special Intensive RevisionForm 7 · Booth Level Agent · Claims and objectionsMains

An analysis by The Hindu found that 796 Form 7 applications seeking deletion of names, filed with BJP-appointed Booth Level Agents (BLAs) as the “objector” in Mustafabad, were mostly in 25 of the constituency’s 294 polling booths in three neighbourhoods (Nehru Vihar, Dayalpur, Chandu Nagar). They make up over 76% of the 1,044 deletion applications received till 4 November in the claims-and-objections phase of the Special Intensive Revision (SIR); the newspaper reports all were against Muslim electors, and at least 87 electors were living at their registered addresses. The Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) says it did not direct any Form 7 to be filed; Delhi BJP spokesperson Praveen Shankar Kapoor said the BLAs filed none, though the forms name them as objectors. This is the newspaper’s analysis; the party denies it. Derived: 796 of 1,044 is about 76.2%.

📌 Static GK

Form 7 is used to object to a name in the electoral roll or seek its deletion under the Representation of the People Act, 1950. A Booth Level Officer (BLO) is a Booth Level Officer, a government official; a Baloch Liberation Army (BLA) is appointed by a party.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • Form 7; BLO vs BLA.

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

  • 796 of 1,044 applications.

Sanjit “Bunker” Roy, founder of Barefoot College, dies

GS-I SocietySocial work · Rural developmentBarefoot College · Tilonia · Bottom-up approachPrelims

Social activist Sanjit “Bunker” Roy died. He founded the Social Work and Research Centre (Barefoot College) at Tilonia, Rajasthan, in 1972, which he described as “a place for cop-outs, drop-outs and wash-outs” rather than certificate chasers; it let villagers identify their own problems, develop local solutions and manage resources. A Doon School alumnus and St. Stephen’s College graduate (1962–67), he won the national squash championship in 1965 and played three world championships; the 1966 Bihar famine, when he was 21, changed his course. Congress’s Jairam Ramesh called him an “institution builder” who worked with the Rajiv Gandhi government on drinking water, immunisation and adult literacy.

📌 Static GK

Rajasthan’s Tilonia is in Ajmer district. The “barefoot” model trains rural people, including women with little schooling, as solar engineers, midwives and teachers.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • Barefoot College; Tilonia 1972.

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

  • Roy founded Barefoot College.

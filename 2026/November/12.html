Kulgam: three CRPF personnel injured in a close-range attack on a checkpoint

GS-III Internal SecurityJammu and Kashmir · TerrorismCRPF · Checkpoint attack · Lieutenant-GovernorPrelims

Three Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF) personnel of the 46 Battalion were injured when terrorists opened fire from close range at a checkpoint in Kulgam, south Kashmir, around 2.15 p.m. on Wednesday, on a day Lieutenant-Governor Manoj Sinha was to attend an event in the district. One of the injured was reported critical and airlifted to Srinagar. Officials were checking whether the attackers came on a motorcycle. Mr. Sinha called it a “cowardly act”, said security forces had been given a “free hand”, and reviewed security at Lok Bhavan, Srinagar; earlier he had inaugurated the Veshow Festival in Kulgam. The Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) condemned the attack. Earlier incidents this year include the killing of two labourers from Chhattisgarh in Kulgam and the shooting of a police constable in Anantnag, both in July.

📌 Static GK

The Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF) is the largest Central Armed Police Force and works under the Ministry of Home Affairs. Public order is a State subject, but in Jammu and Kashmir the Lieutenant-Governor oversees police and security.

Cabinet approves a ₹3,230 crore museum in the North and South Blocks

GS-I Art and CultureHeritage · Public projectsNorth Block · South Block · Phased implementationPrelims

The Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs approved a ₹3,230 crore museum project in New Delhi, spread over about 1.78 lakh square metres in the iconic North and South Blocks after government offices relocate. It will combine immersive galleries, conservation and storage facilities, landscaping and digital experiences, and will be built in phases between 2026-27 and 2030-31. The first gallery, on adornment, is expected to open in North Block in the second half of 2027; other planned galleries cover architecture and cityscapes, mathematics and astronomy, languages and scripts, faith and spirituality, and trade and ideas. About 1.8 million objects from other museums and private collections have been identified for display. The official statement describes it as the world’s largest display area; that is the Government’s claim.

📌 Static GK

The Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA) approves major projects and subsidies. North and South Blocks were designed by Herbert Baker and built in the 1910s–1920s as part of Lutyens’ Delhi.

GS-II GovernanceImmigration · TourismVisa categories · e-Visa · Electronic Travel AuthorisationPrelims

The Home Ministry says that on 27 August 2026 it cut the main visa categories from 13 to nine and sub-categories from 49 to 23, following the October 2025 rationalisation (main categories 22 to 13, sub-categories 63 to 49). Required fields in the application for all 23 categories have been cut by 65–70% (as reported on 4 November), and a special provision for subject-matter experts was added. Visitor visas now have two sub-categories, V-1 and V-2. e-Visa is processed through Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA) under 13 sub-categories for nationals of 172 countries. IT systems are being updated, and visas will be granted under the new scheme by year-end.

Supreme Court on the OBC creamy layer: parental salary alone cannot decide the status

GS-II PolityReservation · JudiciaryCreamy layer · 1993 Office Memorandum · Non-creamy layer certificateMains

An explainer notes that on 6 November a Bench of Justices P.S. Narasimha and R. Mahadevan reaffirmed its 11 March 2026 judgment, which upheld the 1993 Office Memorandum that excludes parental salary and agricultural income from the income/wealth test for the Other Backward Classes (OBC) non-creamy layer. Parental salary cannot by itself decide creamy-layer status; the status and category of the parent’s post are also relevant. The court dismissed the Union’s appeals and told it to reconsider the claims of the candidates. On the Centre’s application (through Solicitor General Tushar Mehta) the Bench said the Union erred in assuming that applying the March ruling retrospectively would have far-reaching consequences for recruitment, cadre allocation, seniority and admissions. The case arose from Civil Services Examination candidates denied OBC non-creamy layer consideration. Update on 11 Nov: that day’s headline-only editorial on consistent creamy-layer exclusion relates to this issue.

📌 Static GK

The creamy layer concept comes from Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (1992), and the 1993 Office Memorandum fixed the criteria. OBC reservation of 27% in Union jobs stems from the Mandal Commission.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • Indra Sawhney 1992; 1993 OM.

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

  • Supreme Court (SC) reaffirmed 11 March 2026 ruling.

Karnataka moves the Supreme Court against quashing of the higher SC/ST quota

GS-II PolityReservation · 50% cap · Ninth Schedule50% ceiling · Ninth Schedule · Nagamohan Das CommissionMains

Karnataka filed a Special Leave Petition (SLP) against the High Court’s 12 October ruling that quashed raising the Scheduled Caste quota from 15% to 17% and the Scheduled Tribe quota from 3% to 7%. The High Court held the 2022 Act ultra vires because it took total reservation to 56%, breaching the 50% cap set by the Supreme Court in 1992, and struck down Sections 3 and 4. The State’s matrix gives SCs 15%, STs 3% and OBCs 32%, which totals 50%. The Chief Minister’s Office says the Congress government will continue to press the Centre to include the law in the Ninth Schedule; a resolution to that effect was moved on 18 December 2025. It also says that when recruitment for 56,432 posts was allowed in February 2026, a 6% backlog reservation for Supreme Court (SC)/STs was protected. The law followed a commission headed by retired judge H.N. Nagamohan Das.

📌 Static GK

Laws in the Ninth Schedule (added by the First Amendment, 1951) are shielded from challenge, but I.R. Coelho (2007) held that those enacted after 24 April 1973 can be reviewed for violating the basic structure. An SLP is filed under Article 136.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • Article 136; I.R. Coelho 2007.

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

  • SC 15 to 17%; ST 3 to 7%.

Data analysis: nearly 70% of stations recorded “severe” PM2.5 during Deepavali

GS-III EnvironmentAir quality · Data transparencyCAAQMS · PM2.5 · Data gaps · Green crackersMains

An analysis of the Central Pollution Control Board’s (CPCB) 634 Continuous Ambient Air Quality Monitoring Stations (CAAQMS) found that only 571 were live and 551 reported PM2.5 at least once in the Deepavali window (5 p.m. on 7 November to 5 a.m. on 9 November). Of these, 375 (about 68%, described as “nearly 70%”; derived) recorded at least 250 µg/m³ (the “severe” band), against 96 in the pre-Deepavali window; this included all 59 Delhi stations. Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan, Haryana, Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat and Andhra Pradesh each had more than two-thirds of stations in that band. Tamil Nadu had 37 stations but only nine reported. Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) said its analysers are calibrated for up to 1,000 µg/m³, so values above that “may appear as a data gap”; the newspaper says some stations went silent before reaching about 1,000, so the clarification may not fully explain the gaps. Delhi, Mumbai and Kolkata showed no drop in sulphur dioxide (SO2) in five years. Update on 10 and 11 Nov: follows the Deepavali pollution and missing-data cards.

📌 Static GK

PM2.5 is particulate matter under 2.5 micrometres. The CPCB was set up in 1974 under the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act and works under the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • CPCB 1974; PM2.5.

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

  • 375 of 551 stations ≥250.

Opinion: how the algorithm is being targeted — a party spokesperson’s argument on takedowns

GS-II PolityFree speech · Social media regulationSection 69A · Sahyog portal · Shreya Singhal · Algorithmic rankingMains

Raviesha Pradeep, spokesperson for the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP), argues that quietly pushing a post down the feed leaves nothing to challenge, unlike a blocked post, which at least triggers a notice. She says the CJP began on 16 May, crossed nine million Instagram followers in four days, and that its X handle was withheld in India on 21 May “in response to a legal demand” (her account). She cites a 10 October letter from the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) asking Meta to review ranking mechanisms so users are not exposed disproportionately to a limited set of narratives. She notes the Karnataka High Court on 24 September 2025 dismissed X Corp’s challenge and called the Sahyog portal “far from being constitutional anathema”, and the Supreme Court’s Shreya Singhal (2015) upheld blocking powers under Section 69A with safeguards (written reasons, hearing, review). She urges Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) and Meta to publish takedown orders. These are the author’s claims; the Government has not responded in the text I read.

📌 Static GK

Section 69A of the Information Technology Act, 2000, lets the Union block online content on grounds such as sovereignty and public order, under the 2009 Blocking Rules. Shreya Singhal v. Union of India (2015) struck down Section 66A.

  • Section 69A; Shreya Singhal 2015.

Explainer: gravitational-wave astronomy is entering its statistical era

GS-III Science and TechnologyAstrophysics · Black holesLIGO · GW250114 · Chirp and ringdownPrelims

On 14 January 2025 the twin LIGO observatories detected GW250114, from two black holes of about 34 and 32 solar masses merging 1.3 billion light-years away, the clearest signal recorded so far. Gravitational waves were first studied in 2015; after a decade of upgrades, scientists can now study hundreds of mergers together to ask how black holes form and whether gravity always behaves as Einstein predicted. A merger produces a rising “chirp” as the black holes orbit faster, then a “ringdown” as the merged hole settles, like a struck bell. LIGO, Virgo and KAGRA are the detectors.

📌 Static GK

Gravitational waves are ripples in spacetime predicted by Einstein’s general theory of relativity (1915); the first detection (GW150914) was announced in 2016. India is building LIGO-India in Maharashtra’s Hingoli district.

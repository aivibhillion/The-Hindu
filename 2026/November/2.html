INDIA bloc’s Ramlila Maidan rally targets the CEC; BJP stages counter-march

GS-II PolityElection Commission · Opposition politicsChalo Dilli · SIR · Article 19(1)(b)Mains

At the Indian National Developmental, Inclusive Alliance (INDIA) rally at Ramlila Maidan on Sunday (1 November), Leader of the Opposition Rahul Gandhi accused Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar of “treason” by “stealing the voice of citizens” and said he would be removed and “go to jail”; these are his statements. Samajwadi Party chief Akhilesh Yadav called the ground the “Haldighati of democracy”. Congress leader K.C. Venugopal called the turnout unprecedented without giving a number. The Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) disputed this: Sambit Patra said about 40% of the ground was empty, with 5,000 to 7,000 of 28,000 chairs vacant at one time, questioning a Congress claim of 10 lakh people; BJP leader J.P. 📌 Static GK

The right to assemble peacefully and without arms is a fundamental right under Article 19(1)(b), subject to reasonable restrictions in the interest of public order (Article 19(3)). The Chief Election Commissioner (CEC)’s removal needs a parliamentary process under Article 324(5).

CJI Surya Kant flags “growing tendency” to seek early hearings of High Court cases in the Supreme Court

GS-II PolityJudiciary · PendencyArticle 226 · Article 32 · Judicial vacanciesMains

Speaking at the platinum jubilee of the Kerala High Court, Chief Justice of India Surya Kant said litigants increasingly ask the Supreme Court for urgent listing of cases pending in High Courts, and asked whether urgent matters could be identified better, listing made predictable and technology used to prioritise cases where delay causes irreversible prejudice. He added that the powers of High Courts under Article 226 exceed the Supreme Court’s under Article 32. Pendency: 65,54,788 cases in High Courts and 97,201 in the Supreme Court, close to one lakh, despite the judge strength rising from 34 to 38 including the CJI. The 25 High Courts have 292 vacancies against a sanctioned strength of 1,122 (as on 6 October 2026).

📌 Static GK

Article 32 is the right to move the Supreme Court for enforcement of fundamental rights; Article 226 lets High Courts issue writs for fundamental rights and “any other purpose”. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar called Article 32 the “heart and soul” of the Constitution.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • Articles 32 and 226; pendency.

Bengaluru’s final electoral rolls show garbled and incorrect entries

GS-II PolityElection Commission · Electoral rollsFinal roll · Transliteration · BLOPrelims

The Hindu found discrepancies in at least 1,000 entries of Bengaluru’s final electoral rolls after the Special Intensive Revision (SIR): names shown as “XXX” or “??????K”, relatives’ names entered as “Husband Husband” or “Wife”, single letters, and even an email address. Assistant Electoral Registration Officers and Booth Level Officer supervisors said they were unaware of the cases and would look into them. A polling official attributed it to a transliteration error: some fonts use special codes for Kannada letters, and if the computer cannot recognise the font while converting a file, the letters appear as unrelated symbols. This explanation is the official’s; it has not been independently verified.

📌 Static GK

A Booth Level Officer (BLO) prepares and verifies the roll at the polling-station level; an AERO assists the Electoral Registration Officer. The roll is revised under the Representation of the People Act, 1950.

Cyclone Murasu: red alert for five districts as landfall is expected on 3 November

GS-I GeographyCyclones · Disaster managementCyclone Murasu · Red alert · APSDMAPrelims

Barely a week after “very severe” Cyclone Arnab, a system over the Bay of Bengal intensified into a cyclonic storm named Murasu (a Tamil name meaning “war drums”, from the regional list) and was about 350 km east of Chennai on Sunday night. It is expected to become a severe cyclonic storm by Monday morning and cross the coast between Nellore and Narsapur between Monday night and early Tuesday, with winds of 100–110 km/h gusting to 120 km/h. The India Meteorological Department (IMD) put Nellore, Prakasam, Guntur, Bapatla and Krishna districts on red alert for 2 November; power utilities say the crossing is likely near Bapatla on 3 November. The State disaster authority listed 20 mandals at risk of extremely heavy rain (nine in Eluru, five each in Krishna and NTR, one in Bapatla). Odisha identified 150 landslide-prone sites in Gajapati district. Update on Arnab: it crossed the coast near Narsapur on 28 October with gusts up to 150 km/h; The Hindu now reports five deaths, crops submerged on over 3.5 lakh hectares, about 2,425 km of roads and 15,000 electric poles damaged, and State losses of ₹4,600 crore. Earlier digests carried other figures; this is the latest report. Preparations: 1,688 medical teams, 116 relief camps, 13,165 emergency beds and 43,672 anti-snake-venom injections; Chief Minister Naidu asked Tamil Nadu and Karnataka for power-restoration crews.

📌 Static GK

Cyclone names in the North Indian Ocean come from a list agreed by member countries of the WMO/ESCAP Panel, assigned in rotation. The IMD’s red alert means take action; orange means be prepared; yellow means watch.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • Cyclone naming; IMD colour codes.

Urban local body polls: 60.36% turnout in 92 bodies; repolling in four wards today

GS-II PolityLocal government · State Election CommissionTurnout · Repolling · Article 243ZAPrelims

Polling in 13 corporations and 79 municipalities and nagar panchayats ended without major law-and-order problems, barring stone-pelting and vehicle burning in Pulivendula. The State Election Commission tentatively put turnout at 60.36%: 58,48,040 of 96,89,061 voters. Highest: Kanigiri (83.86%) and K. Gudur (83.73%); lowest: Greater Visakhapatnam (50.02%). Editorial: support falls for the UN resolution against the U.S. embargo on Cuba

GS-II International RelationsAmericas · United NationsUNGA resolution · Embargo · Global SouthMains

The UN General Assembly resolution on “ending the economic, commercial and financial embargo” on Cuba was passed on 30 October, but with 130 in favour, 25 abstentions and 17 against, down from 165 in favour in 2025 and over 180 of the 193 members for the preceding decade. The editorial links the drop to U.S. pressure, including after the January 2026 operation in Venezuela in which U.S. marines took President Maduro to the U.S., and notes Costa Rica, Ecuador, Argentina and Colombia switched votes, while Germany and Italy moved to abstain for the first time in years, citing Cuba’s ties with Russia. India kept its support and called for “dialogue and diplomacy”; the BRICS New Delhi declaration a month earlier expressed “deep concern” over the blockade. These are the editorial’s interpretations.

📌 Static GK

United Nations General Assembly (UNGA) resolutions are recommendatory, not binding, unlike UN Security Council resolutions under Chapter VII. The U.S. embargo on Cuba dates from the early 1960s; the UNGA has voted on it nearly every year since 1992.

U.S. midterms on 3 November: what is at stake and why the count worries officials

GS-II International RelationsUSA · ElectionsMidterms · Certification · Election Assistance CommissionPrelims

Americans vote on Tuesday (3 November) to elect the Congress for the last two years of Donald Trump’s presidency. Republicans hold 218 of 435 House seats (Democrats 214) and 53 of 100 Senate seats against 45 (two independents caucus with Democrats). A forecasting model cited by The Hindu suggests Democrats could win 230 House seats and 18 of the 35 Senate seats up for election. A separate op-ed by retired Brigadier Anil Raman warns that the U.S. count is run by thousands of county officials and can take days; 26 States order an automatic recount on narrow margins; the Justice Department has sent about 1,000 monitors, and the Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe was not invited for the first time since 2002. Results are certified through November and December, and the new Congress sits on 3 January 2027. These are the writers’ views and forecasts.

📌 Static GK

The entire House (two-year terms) and about one-third of the Senate (six-year terms) face election in the midterms. A majority needs 218 in the House and 51 in the Senate (or 50 with the Vice President’s tie-break).

El Niño and the monsoon

Why even a strong El Niño does not guarantee a poor Indian monsoon

GS-I GeographyMonsoon · ClimateEl Niño · Indian Ocean Dipole · Oceanic Niño IndexPrelims

The 2026 southwest monsoon ended about 13% below the long-term average of 87 cm, and East and Northeast India had their driest season since 1901, with a “ferocious” El Niño expected to peak in December. Of seven monsoons since 1950 that met a moderate or strong El Niño, five (including 2026) were at least 10% below average (“deficient”); only 1997 was average. The Oceanic Niño Index was 1.64°C in 1997, 1.59°C in 2015 and 1.98°C this year. In 1997, a positive Indian Ocean Dipole offset El Niño; in 2015 it barely moved. A 2024 study linked this to winds around Antarctica. The India Meteorological Department (IMD) forecast 92% ±5 for 2026 and 96% ±5 for 2023 (actual 94.7%).

📌 Static GK

El Niño is the warming of the central-eastern equatorial Pacific; La Niña is cooling. A positive Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD) means a warmer western and cooler eastern Indian Ocean, usually favourable to the Indian monsoon.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • El Niño; IOD; ONI.

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

  • 2026 monsoon – 13% below.

Editorial: unemployment falls, but “decent work” remains scarce

Using the Periodic Labour Force Survey rounds of 2017-18 and 2023-24, the authors built a Decent Work Index (employment status, opportunity and social security) across five industries. The overall index rose from 0.247 to 0.302, which they call “alarmingly low”. Manufacturing rose from 0.324 to 0.411 and transport from 0.285 to 0.35; agriculture, still the largest employer, rose from 0.211 to 0.267. Women from Scheduled Tribes and Other Backward Classes saw a decline. Goa, Keralam, Tamil Nadu and Haryana do better; Bihar, Jharkhand and Uttar Pradesh lag. Nearly 90% of the workforce is informal (India Employment Report 2024). The authors urge portable social protection for gig and informal workers, linking incentives to worker protection and intersectional policy.

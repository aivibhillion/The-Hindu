ECI: voters ousted in the SIR in six States and Delhi can resubmit enumeration forms

GS-II PolityElection Commission · Electoral rollsASDD · Enumeration form · Form 6 vs Form 8Prelims

The Election Commission of India (ECI) directed Chief Electoral Officers of Delhi, Maharashtra, Haryana, Andhra Pradesh, Jharkhand, Telangana and Nagaland that electors marked Absent, Shifted, Dead or Duplicate (ASDD) during the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) can submit the enumeration form again, with a statement explaining why they could not do so earlier, instead of using Form 6, which is meant for new registration and carries a declaration of no registration elsewhere. Those who moved within the State must give the new address and Assembly Constituency. Booth-wise ASDD lists are to be displayed afresh at polling stations with probable reasons, and partially filled forms will be regenerated in the Electoral Registration Officer dashboard on ECINET. The move follows the Supreme Court asking, on 28 October, why Form 6 and not Form 8 was being used (petition by Anjali Bhardwaj and Amrita Johri). Activists said that in Delhi, of 47.56 lakh deleted in the first phase, 31.59 lakh were marked permanently shifted and 11.73 lakh absent or untraceable (their figures; the remaining 4.24 lakh is derived). Claim: Congress leader Jairam Ramesh called it a belated admission of wrongful deletion and said the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) had “robbed over 13 crore Indians” of the vote; this is his allegation, not an Election Commission of India (ECI) figure.

📌 Static GK

Form 6 is for new voters and Form 8 is for corrections and shifting of residence. The ECI is constitutional under Article 324; the electoral-roll law is the Representation of the People Act, 1950.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • Form 6 vs Form 8; Article 324.

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

  • ASDD voters can resubmit enumeration forms.

Supreme Court: creamy-layer ruling is not retrospective but governs pending service allocation

GS-II PolityReservation · OBC creamy layerCreamy layer · 1993 Office Memorandum · Civil Services Examination 2025Mains

A Bench of Justices P.S. Narasimha and R. Mahadevan clarified that its 11 March 2026 judgment, upholding a 1993 Office Memorandum that excludes parental salary and agricultural income from the income and wealth test for the Other Backward Classes (OBC) non-creamy layer, does not call for a general revision of service allocations or seniority across earlier recruitment years, and gives no relief to people who were not parties. The Centre (Solicitor General Tushar Mehta) had argued that retrospective effect would affect more than 6.69 lakh reserved vacancies, including 3.7 lakh OBC vacancies, filled over ten years. The Court said the Centre had erred in that assumption. But since services for Civil Services Examination (CSE) 2025, whose result came out on 6 March, were still not allocated, there was no reason to defer the judgment, and it will govern allocation of OBC candidates selected through CSE-2025. The Centre remains free to frame policy on reservation and the creamy layer in line with the Constitution.

📌 Static GK

The creamy layer concept comes from Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (1992), which upheld 27% OBC reservation but excluded the “creamy layer”. Article 16(4) allows reservation in public posts for backward classes not adequately represented.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • Indra Sawhney 1992; Article 16(4).

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

  • 6.69 lakh vacancies cited by the Centre.

Supreme Court calls curbs on vehicles and construction “failed tools” as Delhi AQI crosses 400

GS-III EnvironmentAir pollution · JudiciaryGRAP-III · CAQM · Air Quality IndexMains

A three-judge Bench headed by the Chief Justice asked the Centre whether it has scientific solutions to Delhi’s pollution, saying curbs on vehicles and construction were “failed tools”. The Chief Justice said it was “free for all” on the capital’s roads and asked how fuel and vehicle condition can be checked. Justice Joymalya Bagchi addressed the Commission for Air Quality Management (CAQM), and the Centre’s counsel said CAQM measures were not a complete failure, citing urbanisation, festive activities and stubble burning that make control “uphill”. The Court said the “commodification and commercialisation” of Deepavali by affluent people worsens pollution, with the poorest bearing the worst consequences. Hours later, Delhi moved to Stage III of the Graded Response Action Plan (GRAP-III) as the Air Quality Index rose above 400. These are statements made in court.

📌 Static GK

The Commission for Air Quality Management in the National Capital Region and Adjoining Areas was set up by a 2021 law. GRAP has four stages: I (poor), II (very poor), III (severe), IV (severe plus). An Air Quality Index (AQI) above 400 is “severe”.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • CAQM; GRAP stages.

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

  • GRAP-III at AQI above 400.

Gauhati High Court quashes NSA detention of Kaziranga land-rights activist Pranab Doley

GS-II PolityPreventive detention · Civil libertiesNational Security Act · Proximity of grounds · KazirangaMains

The Gauhati High Court declared the preventive detention of activist Pranab Doley, 40, under the National Security Act (NSA) illegal, holding that the grounds cited lacked the required proximity. He was freed from Golaghat Jail after 118 days. Doley, convenor of the Greater Kaziranga Land and Human Rights Protection Committee, was arrested on 12 July after a protest against a luxury hotel project at Ingle Pathar near Kaziranga National Park. A Golaghat court granted bail on 29 July, and the State invoked the NSA a day later. Police allegations: he trespassed on the site carrying weapons; the State also alleged suspicious foreign transactions in violation of the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act. Indigenous communities opposing the project say it threatens land and livelihoods.

📌 Static GK

The National Security Act, 1980 allows preventive detention up to 12 months. Article 22(3) permits preventive detention, with an Advisory Board review under Article 22(4).

Centre mandates weekly declaration of rice and paddy stocks; doubles Open Market Sale target

GS-III EconomyFood security · Price controlEssential Commodities Act · Food Stock Portal · Bharat RicePrelims

With rice prices rising after a deficient and uneven monsoon, a 6 November order requires traders, retailers, wholesalers, processors and millers to declare stocks of six categories (Basmati, non-Basmati premium, non-Basmati, parboiled, broken rice and paddy) on the Food Stock Portal every Friday. Unregistered units or those breaking stock limits face action under Sections 6 and 7 of the Essential Commodities Act, 1955. The Centre also allowed Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu and Telangana to impose stock limits through control orders until 31 January 2027, is giving extra rice to NAFED, NCCF and Kendriya Bhandar for sale as Bharat Rice, and doubled the e-auction target under the Open Market Sale Scheme (Domestic) for 2026-27 from 25 lakh to 50 lakh tonnes. About 25 lakh tonnes had been sold by 31 October, against 11 lakh tonnes a year earlier.

📌 Static GK

The Food Corporation of India buys and stores foodgrain. The Essential Commodities Act, 1955 lets the Centre regulate production, supply and distribution of listed items, including foodstuffs.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • Essential Commodities Act 1955.

Ground Zero: the Siang dam and a threatened homeland

GS-III InfrastructureHydropower · Tribal rightsSiang Upper Multipurpose Project · Adi · Pre-Feasibility ReportMains

The Siang Upper Multipurpose Project (SUMP), an 11,000 MW mega-dam proposed by the National Hydroelectric Power Corporation (NHPC) in Arunachal Pradesh, was declared a national project in 2008. The Adi community, organised in the Siang Indigenous Farmers’ Forum since about 2012, opposes even the Pre-Feasibility Report (PFR) study, saying the dam threatens paddy and orange farming and ancestral sites; the State argues the study is needed to counter China’s Medog dam on the Yarlung-Tsangpo. Events reported: 4 October, elderly protesters at Uggeng were allegedly lathi charged and 54 were injured; 9 October, activists from Geku arrested under the Arunachal Pradesh Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, 2014; 23 October, a march in Pasighat was allegedly met with lathi charge and tear gas and ended with the NHPC premises set on fire. On 5 November the Forum rejected talks in Itanagar until arrested people are freed and security forces withdrawn. Allegations: villagers claim consent was obtained through MoUs by pressure; the Forum’s youth leader estimates 27 to 40 villages are affected (his estimate). This is a report on the protesters’ side.

📌 Static GK

The Siang is the Brahmaputra’s name in Arunachal Pradesh; it is the Yarlung Tsangpo in Tibet. The Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006 requires consent of gram sabhas in certain cases.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • Siang = Yarlung Tsangpo; FRA 2006.

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

Karnataka claims petrol samples had up to 31.2% ethanol; oil companies dispute the tests

GS-III EconomyEnergy · Ethanol blendingE20 · Legal Metrology · OverblendingMains

Karnataka Food and Civil Supplies Minister Rizwan Arshad said petrol samples taken by the Legal Metrology Department from Bengaluru Urban, Bengaluru Rural, Kolar, Chikkaballapura, Tumakuru and Dakshina Kannada districts had ethanol far above the 20% blend: 31.2% (Indian Oil), 30.5% (Hindustan Petroleum), 26.5% (Bharat Petroleum), 26.1% (Nayara) and 25.5% (Shell), as tested at labs accredited by the National Accreditation Board for Testing and Calibration Laboratories (NABL). He said the companies’ in-house reports did not disclose ethanol content. State claims: 99% of India’s over 35 crore vehicles are not built for blends above 20%; a 10% mileage loss at ₹100 a litre would cost consumers about ₹62,000 crore (his assumption on 6,200 crore litres of annual fuel use), and about ₹1 lakh crore with repair costs, a figure he did not explain. Dispute: the oil marketing companies said the tests did not follow statutory and technical procedures and asked for sampling documents and samples for retesting. These claims are unverified.

📌 Static GK

E20 means petrol with 20% ethanol. Ethanol is largely made from sugarcane and grain under the National Policy on Biofuels. The Legal Metrology Act, 2009 governs weights, measures and labelling.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • E20; Legal Metrology Act 2009.

Feature: women artisans drive Hupari’s ₹1,000-crore silver industry

GS-I SocietyWomen · Crafts · Informal workHupari · Ghungroos · Home-based workMains

A report from Hupari, a town of about 40,000 in Kolhapur district known as Maharashtra’s “Silver Capital”, says women make up 60 to 70% of its roughly 25,000 artisans, many working from home on delicate tasks such as attaching ghungroos (tiny beads of anklets), in an industry with a ₹1,000-crore annual turnover.

Modi: “two historic milestones” — nearly 50 crore out of extreme poverty (World Bank) and an India “freed from Naxalism”

GS-II GovernancePoverty · Tribal welfare · Left-wing extremismJanjatiya Gaurav Divas · Birsa Munda · World BankPrelims

Speaking in New Delhi on Sunday (Janjatiya Gaurav Divas, the birth anniversary of Bhagwan Birsa Munda), Prime Minister Narendra Modi quoted a World Bank report saying nearly 50 crore Indians have come out of extreme poverty and said the country has been freed from Naxalism; he called both “two historic national milestones” that improved the lives of tribal people. He said people affected by Maoism had been “left to their fate” by earlier governments and noted that the country’s leadership is in the hands of “a daughter from the tribal community”, President Droupadi Murmu. Related: the BJP’s criticism of Rahul Gandhi’s absence from the tribute programme (page 11, party allegation).

Birsa Munda (born 15 November 1875) led the Ulgulan (Munda rebellion) in the Chota Nagpur region; Jharkhand was formed on his birth anniversary in 2000, and the Centre declared 15 November Janjatiya Gaurav Divas in 2021. Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups (PVTGs) are 75 groups in India.

Lalit Modi acquires British citizenship, which complicates his extradition to India

GS-II International RelationsCitizenship · ExtraditionCitizenship Act 1955 · Fugitive Economic Offenders ActPrelims

IPL founder Lalit Modi announced on X over the weekend that he had naturalised as a British citizen on 20 September. The loss of Indian citizenship is automatic when a person voluntarily acquires another country’s citizenship, and the paper says this further complicates extradition. He left India in 2010 and is accused in India of financial irregularities, money laundering and violations of the Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA) from his time in the Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI); in 2017 the Enforcement Directorate (ED) asked U.K. authorities to transfer him to face a non-bailable warrant. New Delhi has pressed the U.K. for the return of Lalit Modi, Nirav Modi and Vijay Mallya; Prime Minister Modi raised “economic offenders and fugitives” with then British Prime Minister Keir Starmer in July 2025. He noted that 14 November was King Charles III’s birthday.

📌 Static GK

Section 9 of the Citizenship Act, 1955 provides that an Indian citizen who voluntarily acquires the citizenship of another country ceases to be an Indian citizen. India has an extradition treaty with the U.K. (1992); the Extradition Act, 1962 governs requests, and the Fugitive Economic Offenders Act, 2018 allows confiscation of property.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • Citizenship Act 1955 s.9 (termination); Extradition Act 1962; FEOA 2018.

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

  • Fugitives named: Lalit Modi, Nirav Modi, Vijay Mallya.

West Bengal: fifth “police encounter” death in six months of BJP government; a civil rights body calls them extrajudicial killings

GS-II PolityPolice · Fundamental rightsEncounters · Article 21 · PUCL guidelinesMains

India votes against a UN General Assembly resolution urging a moratorium on the death penalty: 133 in favour, 33 against, 16 abstentions

GS-II International RelationsUN · Human rightsUNGA resolution · Mexico · MoratoriumPrelims

The 193-member UN General Assembly adopted, by recorded vote on Friday, a resolution introduced by Mexico on a “Moratorium on the use of the death penalty”: 133 for, 33 against and 16 abstaining. India voted against. The text calls on all states to establish a moratorium on executions with a view to abolishing the death penalty, expresses deep concern at a significant surge in reported executions in 2025, and also reaffirms the right of every country to develop its own legal system, including legal penalties, in accordance with international law.

📌 Static GK

In Bachan Singh v. State of Punjab (1980) the Supreme Court upheld the death penalty but limited it to the “rarest of rare” cases. A resolution of the General Assembly is not legally binding.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • Bachan Singh 1980: “rarest of rare”; UNGA resolutions are recommendatory.

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

  • Article 72 (President’s pardon) and Article 161 (Governor).

Depression in the Bay may become a very severe cyclonic storm with gusts up to 145 kmph; heavy rain forecast for coastal A.P. on Thursday

GS-I GeographyCyclones · Disaster managementIMD classification · Cyclonic storm · Very severePrelims

Update on 15 Nov (severe cyclonic storm by 18 Nov, 105 kmph): a well-marked low-pressure area over the Bay of Bengal has intensified into a depression and may become a very severe cyclonic storm with gusts up to 145 kmph. The Regional Meteorological Centre (RMC), Chennai, expects a cyclonic storm on Monday (16 Nov) over the central south and adjoining west-central Bay and a severe cyclonic storm on Tuesday. A.P. is not forecast to get rain until 18 November, but squally winds of 40–50 kmph may prevail along the coasts of A.P., northern Tamil Nadu and Odisha from Monday, and in A.P. may rise to 145 kmph on Thursday night. The IMD forecasts scattered extremely heavy rain on Thursday in Kakinada, Dr. B.R. Ambedkar Konaseema, Krishna and Bapatla districts and very heavy rain in the northern districts. Chennai, Tiruvallur and Chengalpattu may get heavy rain on Tuesday and heavy to very heavy rain on Wednesday. Conflict across papers: the intensity forecast has risen on each day (cyclonic storm on 14 Nov, severe with 105 kmph on 15 Nov, possibly very severe with 145 kmph on 16 Nov). Related: Cyclones Arnab and Murasu in the tracker.

📌 Static GK

IMD classes by sustained wind: depression 17–27 knots; deep depression 28–33; cyclonic storm 34–47; severe 48–63; very severe 64–89 (about 118–166 kmph); extremely severe 90–119; super cyclonic storm 120 knots or more. Names are given by a rotating list of 13 North Indian Ocean countries.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • IMD classes: depression → deep depression → cyclonic storm → severe → very severe → extremely severe → super.

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

  • Cyclone-prone coast: A.P. has about 974 km of coastline (second longest after Gujarat).

Opinion: “AI and the consciousness illusion” — intelligence, language and consciousness need not imply each other

GS-III Science and TechnologyOp-edLarge language models · ConsciousnessMains

Disclosure: this printed opinion mentions Anthropic, the company that makes Claude, and its chief executive Dario Amodei, so read it as the author’s view, not mine. The columnist (E. Jacob Koshy) writes that the main reason people baulk at forecasts about AI is large language models’ felicity with language, but that the past century and a half showed consciousness, language and intelligence need not imply each other: George Boole (1854) expressed linguistic reasoning in mathematics and Claude Shannon (1937) showed circuits could embody it. He cites a Nature commentary by Anil Seth, Ned Block and Giulio Tononi arguing that silicon-based AI is unlikely to be conscious, and that the cerebellum holds four-fifths of the brain’s neurons yet its removal does not directly affect consciousness. He concludes that for now “we only have the human to fear”, comparing AI hype to nuclear power, which supplies only about a tenth of the world’s electricity.

Large language models predict the next token from patterns in training text. The Turing test (1950) asked whether a machine’s conversation can be told from a human’s.

Delhi after Deepavali: PM2.5 up to 200 times the WHO limit; data missing at the peak

GS-III EnvironmentAir pollution · GovernancePM2.5 · WHO limit · Stubble burning · Missing dataMains

After Delhiites burst crackers well past the Supreme Court’s 10 p.m. deadline, PM2.5 reached 2,597 µg/m³ at Patparganj (about 173 times the WHO 24-hour limit of 15 µg/m³) at 1 a.m. and 3,008.4 µg/m³ at NSUT West Campus (about 200.5 times) at 5 a.m. on Monday (multiples as stated in the report). Data were missing from several government monitoring stations at the peak, which experts say makes air look better than it was. The 24-hour Air Quality Index (AQI) was 344 (“very poor”) at 4 p.m. on Monday, against 376 a day earlier. Only green crackers were allowed in fixed windows on 7 and 8 November, but traditional crackers were sold. Gufran Beig said winds changed from north-westerly to easterly by Sunday afternoon, limiting stubble smoke; Punjab recorded 1,152 stubble fires on Sunday (the season’s highest), 198 on Monday and 4,657 in total (second lowest since 2020), with a 5.1% contribution to Delhi’s PM2.5 on Sunday. Allegations: Aam Aadmi Party (AAP) called the missing data “data fraud”; Delhi Minister Kapil Mishra alleged an AAP-Punjab “conspiracy” over stubble fires. Update on the 7 and 8 Nov digests (AQI near 400, then 362).

📌 Static GK

PM2.5 is particulate matter under 2.5 micrometres that can enter the bloodstream. India’s National Ambient Air Quality Standards are set by the Central Pollution Control Board; the WHO limits are stricter.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • PM2.5; NAAQS.

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

  • AQI 344 on Monday; 200 times WHO limit.

Supreme Court: commercial crop cultivation in reserve forests is not protected by the Forest Rights Act

GS-III EnvironmentForests · Tribal rightsForest Rights Act 2006 · Minor forest produce · AgasthyamalaiMains

A Bench of Justices Vikram Nath and Sandeep Mehta, in a 6 November order on extensive silk cotton cultivation inside reserve forests of the Agasthyamalai Biosphere Reserve, made clear that commercial cultivators and plantation owners are distinct from forest-dwelling Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers protected under the Forest Rights Act (FRA), 2006. The Court directed the Central Empowered Committee to examine Tamil Nadu’s opinion that silk cotton is “minor forest produce” and recorded a prima facie view against the State’s claim of historical cultivation. “If commercial crops are permitted to be cultivated in the forest areas, the very nature of the forest is bound to change,” Justice Mehta said.

📌 Static GK

The Forest Rights Act, 2006 recognises individual and community rights of forest dwellers; it is administered by the Ministry of Tribal Affairs. A biosphere reserve is a UNESCO-recognised area combining conservation and sustainable use.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • FRA 2006; biosphere reserve.

APPSC / TGPSC 🏛️ Exam angle

  • Commercial crops not covered.

Andhra Pradesh overhauls fireworks safety after Rayavaram and Vetlapalem explosions

GS-III Disaster ManagementIndustrial safety · RegulationPESO · Risk-based inspections · State nodal authorityMains

The State government overhauled its fireworks safety, licensing and enforcement framework after explosions at cracker units in Rayavaram and Vetlapalem, adding centralised monitoring, risk-based inspections and stricter accountability. The Director General of the A.P. State Disaster Response and Fire Services is the State Nodal Authority, Collectors oversee implementation with quarterly reviews, and a State Explosives and Fireworks Safety Cell will be formed within 30 days to keep a digital register of units, schedule inspections and coordinate with the Petroleum and Explosives Safety Organisation (PESO). A separate report says Deepavali on 8 November, 19 days later than in 2025, pushed Visakhapatnam and Rajamahendravaram into the “poor” Air Quality Index (AQI) band, and night noise reached 111 dB(A) in Visakhapatnam and 112 dB(A) in Vijayawada against a 45 dB(A) night standard for residential areas.

📌 Static GK

PESO regulates explosives under the Explosives Act, 1884 and the Explosives Rules, 2008. Noise limits are set under the Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000.

UPSC 🎯 Prelims pointers

  • Explosives Act 1884; PESO.

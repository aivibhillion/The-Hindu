Collagen supplements: reviews behind skin claims contain statistical errors, an investigation finds

A Health-page feature by Monisha Madhumita reports a recent investigation by Grimes and Mahto of two reviews supporting collagen supplements for skin: a 2021 systematic review in the International Journal of Dermatology (19 studies, 1,125 participants) and a 2023 meta-analysis in Nutrients (26 randomised trials, 1,721 participants), both reporting better hydration and elasticity. The investigation found standard errors mistaken for standard deviations, studies misclassified by measurement method, results reported as significant when the original authors said otherwise, and pooling of different measures into one analysis. 📌 Static GK

A meta-analysis pools results of several trials; a systematic review collects them by a set method. Standard deviation describes spread in the sample; standard error describes uncertainty of the mean (SE = SD ÷ √n), so mixing them up distorts pooled results.
